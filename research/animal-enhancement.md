# Animal cognitive enhancement above baseline, and adult rescue

## Verdict

**Animals can learn faster, retain memories longer, and sometimes perform better on several memory tasks than healthy controls. Some developmental-disorder phenotypes can also be reversed after development. Neither finding establishes an enhancement-like intervention: rapid, fully reversible, large, broad cognitive enhancement in healthy people.**

The closest animal demonstrations are acute ampakine effects in alert monkeys, peripheral klotho in mice and aged monkeys, systemic IGF-II in rodents, ISR inhibition, and task-linked electrical stimulation. Each misses important parts of the profile. The most dramatic genetic demonstrations usually change development or lasting memory formation, not an on-demand cognitive state. Several successful mouse rescues have failed in human trials.

This is an evidence map, not a treatment proposal. It contains no administration recommendations, sourcing, synthesis, or self-experimentation. Rolf's lack of a wet lab does not change the scientific goal: these findings are leads to evaluate, not interventions currently available to him.

## What was covered and how to read the evidence

Sources were opened through Europe PMC's REST API using `curl` on 4 October 2026. Searches covered the named genetic, systemic, epigenetic, metabolic, and developmental-rescue families, with targeted searches for replications, contradictory findings, and human trials. Selected open full texts were inspected. Every cited source below was opened; the source list distinguishes **A: abstract/record opened** from **F: full text opened**. Opening an abstract verifies what that abstract says, not every claim in an inaccessible paper.

**Coverage is broad but not literally exhaustive.** All intervention families named in the brief are represented. Coverage of individual experiments, species, every disease model, newer clinical programs, and independent replication is partial. This was not an all-record, preregistered systematic review: targeted search results were inspected, but every result of every broad query was not screened. Full-text access failed for a number of older papers; numeric extraction from those papers is therefore unavailable here. Unsuccessful searches are not evidence that a study or replication does not exist.

The distinction between these outcomes is essential:

- **Healthy enhancement:** treated healthy animals outperform comparable untreated healthy animals. A normal young wild-type control is the strongest comparator for Rolf's goal.
- **Age-related rescue:** old animals improve relative to old controls. They may have no diagnosed disease, but this does not demonstrate performance beyond a healthy young baseline.
- **Disease rescue:** a mutant, injured, stressed, or otherwise impaired animal improves. “No longer significantly different from wild type” is not a statistical equivalence test and not proof of complete normalization.
- **Prevention:** treatment during development or before a deficit appears. Testing an animal in adulthood does not make its treatment an adult rescue.
- **Plasticity or neurological rescue only:** restored LTP, visual-cortex plasticity, breathing, locomotion, or sociability is not automatically restored cognition.

In the tables, **NR** means a numerical between-group behavioral effect size was not available in the opened material; it does not mean zero effect or that the full paper contains no quantitative data. Retention intervals and training requirements are reported where useful, but are not standardized effect sizes. A p-value, synapse count, protein concentration, or LTP percentage is not an intelligence effect size. No graph-derived percentages have been invented.

Replication is described rather than collapsed into a yes/no badge. A second cohort in the same paper is an internal repeat; another species from the same investigators is an extension; a different laboratory testing a different intervention at the same pathway is convergence, not direct replication. “Not established” means this review did not verify independent reproduction of the specific claim.

### What survived the famous genetic stories?

**NR2B survived as evidence for task-specific headroom, not as evidence for superintelligence.** The rat paper supplies particularly useful limits: partial-cue advantages depended on the training stage, and a working-memory advantage was not significant at every delay. The reported LTP values, **209 ± 16.82% versus 166 ± 8.67%**, describe synaptic potentiation, not memory performance. It would be misleading to turn those numbers into an IQ gain. [19838302]

**CREB's role is much stronger than the original fly enhancement claim.** The failed replication should be retained alongside the later mammalian positive results. The defensible conclusion is that transcriptional regulation can change consolidation thresholds under particular conditions, not that activating CREB reliably produces single-trial mastery. [15470148] [21677163]

**PKMζ was not simply “debunked,” but the clean original story broke.** The knockout/ZIP experiments undermine interpreting ZIP-induced forgetting as specific proof that PKMζ is indispensable. A 2016 study found compensating PKCι/λ in mutant mice; a 2024 study found KIBRA–PKMζ interactions necessary for its tested persistent memories in wild-type animals. Those studies come substantially from the original research network. They support a more specific maintenance mechanism, not independently established broad enhancement or a universal memory molecule. The gain-of-function finding and the necessity controversy are different claims. [27187150] [38924398]

### What failed in the young-blood story

The defensible result is **systemic biology can influence brain function**, including cognition. Three stronger claims do not follow:

1. That old-to-young damage and young-to-old improvement identify a single beneficial molecule.
2. That more of that molecule is necessarily better. Klotho's nonmonotonic monkey result and GDF11's systemic harms argue directly against this.
3. That biochemical or neurogenesis “rejuvenation” equals broad cognitive rejuvenation.

GDF11's muscle replication failure is not itself a failed replication of a memory experiment; the original CNS paper also did not establish the full cognitive claim often attached to GDF11. Later brain results should be considered on their own merits. Likewise, plasma dilution is a plausible alternative route and mechanistic challenge, not a logical refutation of TIMP2/PF4 sufficiency experiments.

### Why adult rescue matters—and what it does not establish

These experiments reject the strongest fatalistic claim: **a developmental genetic defect does not necessarily fix an animal's adult capabilities permanently.** MeCP2, Ras, mTOR, SynGAP, chromatin regulation and glutamatergic signaling continue to matter in the mature brain. Correcting current dysfunction can unlock function without rebuilding the entire developmental history.

They do **not** establish that every lost function is recoverable. Angelman, SHANK3 and SYNGAP1 show different rescue windows for different endpoints within the same disorder. Rett's dramatic neurological reversal is not an IQ measurement. Human trofinetide is a real positive symptomatic result, but not equivalent to the mouse gene-restoration experiment.

The clinical record is not merely “translation is hard”: selective GABA-A modulation in Down syndrome, mGlu5/arbaclofen programs in fragile X, larger NF1 statin testing, and TSC/PTEN mTOR cognition trials failed their central efficacy claims or did not show broad improvement. Those are substantive negative results. They neither erase the mouse findings nor justify selling the mouse findings as human cognitive restoration.

### The boundary between rescue and enhancement

A treatment can be transformative for an impaired animal while adding nothing to a healthy one. Rescue can improve performance by restoring input, reducing seizures, relieving inflammation, repairing metabolism, or normalizing excessive inhibition. That is evidence of **recoverable function**, not evidence of unlimited above-normal capacity.

Conversely, healthy-animal results should not be dismissed simply because they are not human trials: NR2B, inducible phosphatase regulation, IGF-II, ISRIB, CX717, and selected stimulation experiments demonstrate genuine headroom. What remains **not established** is the conjunction Rolf asked for: **large improvements across learning, memory, reasoning and attention, beginning within hours, fully reversible, in healthy people**. No opened source demonstrates that conjunction. These animal results keep specific biological possibilities open; they do not currently deliver cognitive enhancement.
