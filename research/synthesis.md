# Candidate routes toward large-scale cognition

## Verdict

**The reviewed evidence does not establish an acute, fully reversible, large and broad cognitive enhancement in healthy people. It also does not rule one out.** Real above-baseline animal gains, exceptional human skills and some substantial human task effects leave scientific leads, not an available cognitive enhancement intervention. Neither a measured universal ceiling nor a numerical definition of “large-scale” emerges from these reports.

**Run the public-data plasticity-state experiment first, then the understudied-gene search, then the fixed-resource computational experiment.** This is an order for learning something discriminating overnight, not a ranking of proven treatments. The destination stays large gains across learning, memory, reasoning and attention in healthy people, beginning within minutes to hours, with the enhanced state reversible. A gene list, better memory classifier or successful simulation is not that destination.

## What the reports jointly establish

### There is headroom, but the evidence does not join up into the requested state

Some healthy animals outperform healthy controls after intervention. The strongest counterexample to an indiscriminate “only deficit rescue works” story is Porrino's alert-monkey CX717 experiment: delayed-matching accuracy was **74.9 ± 2.9% versus 90.5 ± 1.3%**, vehicle versus treatment; on difficult trials it was **46.7 ± 4.8% versus 71.7 ± 2.4%**, reported means ± SEM [S4]. These are substantial task gains, not intelligence multipliers. The animal report's human follow-up did not establish consistent prespecified benefit, and other ampakines cannot inherit this experiment's result.

Healthy young mice also show selected gains with klotho/PF4, IGF-II and several genetic manipulations. Adult rescue experiments show that some developmental deficits remain modifiable after development. Neither fact demonstrates broad healthy-human enhancement. Conversely, animal-to-human failures do not erase animal headroom or prove that another mechanism cannot work.

Human extremes provide a different kind of headroom. Mnemonic experts can perform extraordinarily on suitable material, but training and task representation matter. HSAM participants were comparable with controls on most standard laboratory memory tests despite exceptional autobiographical recall [S15]. Comparative brains and computational learning systems show alternative ways to allocate, encode and retrieve information. They do not expose a measured, acutely accessible human reserve.

### The reliable human benchmarks are mostly selective; the large-looking exceptions must stay visible

In healthy, non-sleep-deprived adults, Roberts reports overall **SMD 0.12** for modafinil and **0.21** for methylphenidate, with methylphenidate recall **0.43**, sustained attention **0.42** and inhibitory control **0.27** [S8]. Sleep targeted-memory reactivation yields **g = 0.29, 95% CI 0.21–0.38**, across 91 experiments, strengthening previously learned material [S9]. These are genuine partial effects, not a broad transformed operating mode.

The non-drug report prevents turning that observation into “all human effects are small.” Shaw's acute healthy-human hyperoxia meta-analysis reports memory accuracy **g = 0.34**, reaction speed **0.82**, crystallized intelligence **0.73** and executive function **0.88**, among other endpoints. The same abstract rates the evidence low quality and says prediction intervals often cross zero [S11]. Zhu's photobiomodulation meta-analysis reports global cognition **SMD 0.66, 95% CI 0.23–1.08**, and working-memory span **1.41, 95% CI 0.78–2.04**; it mixes healthy and impaired populations and heterogeneous protocols [S12]. The healthy executive-reaction-time subgroup was nonsignificant. These are published magnitude signals worth auditing, not numbers to soften away or accept uncritically.

A collection of positive domains from different people, protocols and studies is not simultaneous broad enhancement in the same healthy people. SMD, g, percentage correct, retention duration and synapse counts are not interchangeable, and effects from separate interventions cannot be added into a predicted “stack.” None of the reports supplies a valid conversion to cognitive enhancement or IQ points.

### Strong mechanism evidence and useful cognition frequently separate

More plasticity is not necessarily better computation. Nardou demonstrates reopened **social reward learning** in adult mice, not generalized learning. Its transcriptional signature is an association with experimentally defined open/closed conditions [S1]. Acute human psychedelic/ketamine performance in the acute-mechanism report is often impaired; post-acute broad learning-rate enhancement remains an open question, not an established compensating benefit.

ISRIB has an independent healthy-mouse spatial-learning null [S5], and the animal report documents object-place impairment alongside other memory positives. IGF-II has positive local experiments but an independent negative systemic/Angelman study [S6]. Target engagement can also coexist with clinical failure. Iclepertin's phase 3 cognition contrast was **0.127 MCCB T-score points, 95% CI −0.396–0.650**, across the reported CONNEX program [S13]. These findings are reasons to test the actual functional claim, not merely pathway activation.

Nor did every clinical program fail. Xanomeline/trospium improved cognition in a prespecified impaired schizophrenia subgroup, **d = 0.54**, while its full cognitive sample had no significant effect [S14]. Alzheimer antibodies in the history report reduced decline in selected patients. Those successes are real and answer different questions from acute enhancement in rested healthy people.

### Timing, baseline and reversibility require separate evidence

Healthy older participants should not automatically be called impaired. Improvement relative to an age-matched healthy control is enhancement in that population; it does not show superiority to a healthy young baseline. Likewise, a deliberately weak learning protocol is not automatically a diseased brain, but it can make a retention effect look dramatic without measuring a general capacity increase.

Ending stimulation or clearing a substance ends an input, not necessarily the induced state. Conversely, retaining newly learned information does not itself prove the enhanced capacity remained switched on. Reversibility concerns subsequent capacity and unwanted physiological effects, not mandatory erasure of useful memories. The reviewed literature rarely measures that distinction adequately. Klotho's aged-monkey benefit appeared at four hours and remained detectable at 14–23 days; that time course does not establish a fast, fully reversible cognitive switch [S3].

### Public computation can discriminate hypotheses, not certify a human enhancer

The dry-lab report establishes real entry points: cognition summary statistics, gene-knownness resources, atlases, perturbation data and open behavioral/neural data. It does not establish causal directions for acute intervention. Lifelong genetic association can reflect development, health, environment or indirect genetic effects. The successful computational-to-mechanism examples in that report still needed experiments.

The theory report supplies executable comparisons that ask whether better allocation moves several performance measures outward together rather than simply trading one for another. That is a useful question. A model's answer depends on its architecture, information and resource accounting; it is not a human effect-size estimate. In particular, e-prop approaches backpropagation-through-time performance but learns more slowly in the cited paper; it is not a demonstrated super-learning algorithm [S16].

## Where the reports disagree, and how to resolve the disagreement

Their rankings optimize different things. The acute report favors sleep consolidation for demonstrated human effects and plasticity windows for speculative headroom. The non-drug report favors precision stimulation and retains hyperoxia's larger acute signals. The drug-history report ranks familiar stimulants highest within its drug-only evidence. The animal report favors acute ampakines and systemic factors. The theory report ranks reversible control policies for resemblance to an on-demand state, but credit assignment for computational testability. The dry-lab report favors dark genes for unexplained biology. None is a common-scale efficacy league table.

There are also substantive qualifications. The acute report's description of human closed-loop gains as restoration is too broad if extended to every noninvasive network-stimulation study: its corrected Wang discussion and the non-drug report preserve enhancement against healthy comparators. The historical tDCS null does not erase later small pooled positives, while later meta-analyses do not erase the large preregistered memory null. Larger all-population exercise estimates do not supersede healthy-only bias-sensitive estimates. These are differences in populations, protocols and inference, not grounds to average incompatible numbers.

The strongest disagreements are inside the source literature: systemic IGF-II positives versus negative reproduction; healthy ISRIB positives, nulls and task costs; uncertain stimulation transfer; low-certainty large hyperoxia/photobiomodulation estimates versus modest established benchmarks. Retraction is different again: the Stanyer acoustic-sleep meta-analysis's results are invalidated, not simply controversial [S10]. The independent Hu TMR result is a different intervention and remains evidence.

Finally, the acute report's suggested decisive plasticity-window experiment would decide an important **branch**, not the whole cognitive enhancement question. A negative result would not rule out systemic factors, unfamiliar biology, precision state control or another mechanism. Nor do energy budgets, the proposed behavioral information bottleneck or evolutionary trade-offs establish a numerical global ceiling. The five reports and partial animal map do not support an impossibility verdict.

## Six dry-lab routes

These are analysis programs, not administration or device-use proposals. Each can begin with public information on Rolf's M5 Pro MacBook with 24 GB RAM. Their best outputs stop short of human efficacy. Modal requirements below are planning judgments, not measured runtimes or cost quotations.

### Route 1 — Is adult plasticity a shared biological state, or a collection of drug-specific responses?

**Idea.** Test whether a transcriptional program identifies the open learning-window state across different perturbations and follows its closing, rather than merely identifying a drug. This targets unexplained biology behind Nardou's finding without designing molecules. The hard comparison is transfer to a withheld treatment, especially ketamine's open-versus-closed time points, not fitting the published pooled contrast again.

**Data and tools.** GEO **GSE230679** exposes the processed TPM matrix and complete differential-expression results; its SOFT metadata identifies drug, time and critical-period label for the 27 samples. I opened the metadata and matrix header. The authors' public R code documents the original analysis, but contains local paths and references to unavailable cached objects; it is not a ready-to-run pipeline. Use R or Python for a fresh, small, explicitly predictive analysis. TPM is suitable for carefully qualified expression-pattern analysis, not a substitute for raw counts or kallisto bootstraps in reproducing the original inferential pipeline. Human Protein Atlas and orthology tables can later locate conserved candidates. Resource links are below.

**Compute, output and distance to cognitive enhancement.** No Modal is needed for the first matrix analysis; optional small CPU jobs can perform grouped resampling. No GPU or raw-read alignment is needed initially. Best case: a cross-treatment, time-sensitive program with interpretable candidate genes and explicit competing explanations. This gives a biological hypothesis for controllable learning susceptibility. It establishes neither large cognition nor breadth beyond social learning, and its source observations at 48 hours and two weeks are not an hours-scale human on/off state. The eventual bridge would have to demonstrate useful broad learning and acute control, not just reproduce these windows.

**Failure and lesson.** Drug identity, batch, elapsed time or cell-composition shifts may explain the signature. Critical-period labels describe experimental conditions, not each sequenced animal's measured general learning ability. If prediction does not transfer, the simple shared-program interpretation loses support in these data. If batch or design prevents discrimination, the result is non-identifiability, not absent biology. A successful signature can also be downstream repair or compensation rather than a causal lever.

### Route 2 — Find poorly understood human cognition genes without mistaking annotation for causation

**Idea.** Start with human cognitive-performance association, then ask which implicated genes remain poorly characterized and plausibly operate in adult brain or peripheral-to-brain signaling. This is discovery beyond the familiar receptor list. Keep developmental-only explanations visible rather than discarding them and pretending the remaining genes are acute targets.

**Data and tools.** Begin anonymously with Lee cognitive-performance **GCST006572**, Unknome protein/orthologue tables, Pharos canonical target classifications, public gene annotations and a population-matched public LD reference. I opened the GWAS file header: it includes alleles, frequency, beta, SE and p-value. The Catalog lists the sample as **257,841 European-ancestry individuals**. Add HPA adult expression and publicly available brain eQTLs for locus follow-up. MAGMA can provide gene-level association; SuSiE/coloc can investigate selected loci when compatible LD and QTL data are available. Savage intelligence results provide sensitivity/convergence, not automatically independent replication. Genebass cognitive burden results and neuronal perturbation screens are later additions only where the relevant tables and terms are actually accessible.

**Compute, output and distance to cognitive enhancement.** Stream summary statistics locally and use gene/cluster-level tables rather than loading a whole atlas. No Modal is needed for the initial joins and association inventory. Locus-by-locus fine-mapping/colocalization can use parallel CPU containers; no GPU is needed. Best case: an auditable dossier connecting a poorly understood gene to a cognitive locus, a plausible tissue context and a discriminating functional hypothesis. There is no defensible forecast of effect magnitude, acute onset or reversibility. A lifelong protective variant is not a reversible adult intervention.

**Failure and lesson.** Nearest-gene mapping, LD, gene length, annotation bias, cohort overlap or indirect genetic effects can generate apparent convergence. MAGMA, PoPS and locus-to-gene scores derived from the same GWAS are not independent experiments. Low publication count is not evidence of importance; gnomAD constraint is not cognition association. Failure to produce a robust candidate means these resources did not resolve an unknown gene, not that undiscovered cognitive biology is absent. A negative bulk-brain eQTL result can reflect missing cell types or times, not a nonfunctional gene.

### Route 3 — Can a reversible controller improve several computations at fixed resources?

**Idea.** Treat cognitive enhancement as a theory question: can changing allocation and credit routing improve acquisition, retention, reasoning and attention together without supplying extra knowledge, storage or compute? Compare a controllable policy with indiscriminate gain/plasticity increases. This synthesizes the theory report's scheduling, credit assignment and interference-protection hypotheses rather than assuming stronger excitation is the answer.

**Data and tools.** Use openly specified tasks and generated datasets with published seeds: delayed-feedback learning, interfering associative memories, graph-based relational inference and target detection under distraction. Public e-prop examples and continual-learning code are references, not compulsory dependency stacks. NumPy/PyTorch and simple baselines suffice; publish the generators and all resource accounting. Fit human interpretation only to actual public results, not invented human observations.

**Compute, output and distance to cognitive enhancement.** A compact prototype fits locally. Modal can run independent CPU seeds, or one modest GPU job if a measured pilot makes that worthwhile; frontier-model training is unnecessary. Best case: a reproducible outward shift of the joint performance/resource frontier with a controller that can be switched back while learned content remains. That resembles the requested onset and reversibility computationally. It establishes no biological access, safe human implementation, human magnitude or transfer. Neural-network training steps are not human learning hours.

**Failure and lesson.** Gains may disappear against tuned baselines, unfamiliar task structure or honest accounting for the controller's own cost. They may depend on an oracle, hidden pretraining, extra replay, lost old memories or neglected stimuli. Such failures identify what the attractive “unused reserve” story was secretly spending. They reject that model mechanism under its assumptions, not all possible human enhancement.

### Route 4 — Find the missing blood-to-brain relay behind klotho-associated enhancement

**Idea.** Park's result leaves a concrete mechanistic hole: PF4 can reproduce selected benefits, but klotho still works in PF4-knockout mice [S2]. Other messengers, redundancy, developmental compensation and non-platelet explanations remain possible. Analyze the existing perturbations to prioritize these explanations, not assume PF4 is the necessary relay.

**Data and tools.** Public **PXD040167** provides plasma proteomics, including a processed Spectronaut report; **GSE171929** provides hippocampal counts after PF4-related comparisons, and the authors' GitHub supplies R analysis and expression/cognitive-score objects. PRIDE's project metadata specifies CC0. I opened its file list, experimental-design file and the processed report's header. R/DESeq2, streamed protein-table processing, orthologue mapping and HPA expression can connect candidate signals to recipient tissues. Plasma and hippocampal cohorts must not be falsely joined as matched animals or as a measured mediation chain.

**Compute, output and distance to cognitive enhancement.** Processed tables can be analyzed locally; optional CPU containers handle resampling. Do not start by reprocessing all raw mass spectra. Best case: a ranked, falsifiable relay hypothesis consistent with an adult four-hour mouse effect and the observed brain response. That is closer to acute biology than lifelong GWAS association, but currently narrow animal cognition, with no demonstrated healthy-human broad state or rapid state recovery. Persistent learned benefits do not by themselves resolve recovery kinetics.

**Failure and lesson.** The public sample map needs care: the community SDRF labels treatment-named fraction files as biological replicates, while the project methods describe treatment-pooled fractions for library generation. The quantitative report instead has numbered individual-run names. Those are not a verified treatment assignment. PMC supplement downloads encountered a browser challenge; I did not recover that missing mapping. Until resolved from public original records, no treatment labels may be guessed. One can inspect the separately documented PF4 RNA data, but that answers a different question. Even a sound differential-protein result might be a platelet-release or sample-handling marker, not a cognitive messenger. A blocked mapping is an access/provenance failure; a robust nonspecific response weakens the proposed relay, not klotho's entire biology.

### Route 5 — Audit apparent broad human enhancement and build a detector that cannot win by changing the question

**Idea.** Prioritize the unresolved large acute human claims rather than accepting their averages or dismissing them. Ask which effects survive correct sampling units, population separation, endpoint dependence, active controls and speed–accuracy accounting. In parallel, establish what existing datasets could actually detect about joint benefit versus effort or trade-offs.

**Data and tools.** Start with the published hyperoxia and photobiomodulation estimates [S11–S12], then extract accessible primary-study results and supplements, preserving an explicit inaccessible-study list. Their full meta-analytic datasets were not verified open here; the abstracts alone cannot support a numerical reanalysis. An immediately runnable measurement component uses Bowman's public **PECO** participant/session knapsack and CANTAB tables [S20]. I opened its repository, README and table headers. Use R mixed models/metafor or Python equivalents, with study/participant-level dependence and transparent effect-size denominators. Port the published MATLAB calculations where necessary; a proprietary runtime is not required to analyze the CSV data.

**Compute, output and distance to cognitive enhancement.** Laptop CPU is sufficient; Modal adds only parallel bootstrap/sensitivity jobs if needed. Best case: an independently reconstructable human signal worth prospective confirmation, or a clear demonstration of which aspects of the cognitive enhancement claim existing measurements cannot identify. PECO can calibrate performance/effort and cross-task trade-offs; it is not a complete unfamiliar-learning, delayed-memory and reasoning battery and does not establish full reversal. Neither a simulation-injected gain nor a favorable composite is human cognitive enhancement evidence.

**Failure and lesson.** Published effects may be driven by mixed clinical samples, dependent outcomes, inappropriate SD/SE conversions, small studies or selective endpoints. Alternatively, unavailable primary data may prevent adjudication. Missing data must not be filled with guessed correlations or treated as zero effect. A negative PECO reanalysis would support its reported limitations of those interventions, not refute the larger hyperoxia or photobiomodulation claims. Noisy individual response estimates must not become retrospectively selected “super-responders.”

### Route 6 — Test whether accessible brain states can control useful performance, not merely predict it

**Idea.** Distinguish an EEG signature of good memory from a control opportunity. Reanalyze an actual noninvasive closed-loop timing experiment and ask whether choosing when to present material improves useful learning at fixed exposure and elapsed time. This is the closest route here to direct human state-dependent control, without proposing use of stimulation hardware.

**Data and tools.** OpenNeuro **ds004706**, “Spatial memory and non-invasive closed-loop stimulus timing,” exposes read-only training sessions and closed-loop sessions with items presented during predicted good or poor encoding states. **ds004395**, PEERS, supplies complementary healthy memory/EEG data. I opened both CC0 dataset descriptions and READMEs. Rudoler's published abstract reports that the good-minus-poor recall contrast increased with out-of-sample decoder accuracy, not a universal broad enhancement [S21]. Use MNE-BIDS, regularized classifiers and trial-level mixed models. Split by sessions/participants before feature selection, retain all eligible participants, and account for waiting time and missing trials.

**Compute, output and distance to cognitive enhancement.** Stream selected sessions; do not mirror the full EEG corpus. CPU feature extraction and linear models are the starting point, with Modal useful for independent session processing and a larger-memory worker only if a pilot requires it. A GPU is not intrinsic to this question. Best case: a defensible state-dependent timing effect, external-validation limits and a candidate policy for later testing. Acute timing is plausible after calibration, and software input is switchable. The demonstrated construct is selected-memory assistance, not broad intrinsic capacity; multi-day calibration, generalization, useful gain per unit time and physiological reversibility remain separate.

**Failure and lesson.** A decoder may exploit item difficulty, movement, temporal autocorrelation or prior task structure. Good-versus-poor timing can differ without beating ordinary presentation; waiting can erase the gain per minute. Observational PEERS data cannot identify the outcome of an unobserved intervention, and even closed-loop data do not identify arbitrary new policies without treatment support. Failure separates poor observability, lack of causal leverage and absence of net benefit. Do not substitute requested-access RAM stimulation data: the current Penn portal distinguishes open OpenNeuro releases from Box/request-based collections.

## Recommendation and the first three experiments

**My recorded first choice is Route 1.** It has adult perturbations, behavior-defined experimental states, an open-to-closed comparison and a small public matrix. It asks a sharper biological question than another broad “plasticity genes” enrichment. It beats Route 2 for the first run because lifelong genetics has a much longer bridge to acute control; Route 3 because a simulator can manufacture its own reserve; Route 4 because its key plasma sample mapping is unresolved; Route 5 because the large-signal numerical source data are not yet verified accessible; and Route 6 because decoding selected memory is a narrower demonstrated control problem and its EEG pipeline is heavier.

This is not a claim that a days-long mouse plasticity window is the closest intervention to cognitive enhancement. It is the best bounded next analysis of one proposed prerequisite. Its purpose is to find or reject a shared, potentially controllable biological program, then retain the unmet breadth, magnitude, onset and reversal requirements rather than quietly dropping them.

**Fallback order: Route 2 → Route 3 → Route 4 → Route 5 → Route 6.** Route 4 gets only a public-provenance resolution attempt before quantitative treatment comparisons; if the individual-run map remains missing, record it as unavailable and continue. Route 5 can run its public measurement component even if the full anomaly audit remains partial. A finished narrow deliverable also triggers the next route rather than turning into an unlimited optimization project.

### First experiment: withheld-treatment plasticity-state prediction

**Inputs.** Use `GSE230679_20230425_psychadelic_study_tpm_matrix.tsv.gz`, `GSE230679_criticalPeriodDE_all_test_results.tsv.gz`, the series SOFT metadata, Nardou's published state definitions and `SupCode4_RNAseqAnalysis.Rmd`. The directory lists the processed files at 4.3M and 2.0M. The public metadata provides three replicates for each represented drug/time condition. Gene-expression sample names differ from GEO accession names; construct and audit the join from drug, day and replicate, not column position.

**Steps.** First recover the documented design and record missing metadata. The authors' model used RNA-isolation batch plus critical-period state and explicitly omitted time; batch is referenced in the code but was not present in the SOFT characteristics I opened. Exact reproduction of that model is therefore not established from these processed inputs. Do not reconstruct unknown batch labels from the desired outcome.

Next analyze the public TPM patterns with all feature selection, normalization choices learned from data, and model fitting confined to training folds. Use a simple regularized predictor and interpretable gene-set summaries, not a high-capacity network. Withhold complete drug groups, not random individual samples sharing the same treatment. Reserve ketamine's two time points together as a particularly discriminating test: can a program learned from other conditions track the condition that opens and later closes? Report transfer separately for each held-out perturbation and compare expression patterns with the corresponding saline/time controls.

Then evaluate batch uncertainty, condition-level resampling, time-related expression, drug-specific programs and plausible bulk-tissue composition explanations. State is defined by drug and time, so a saturated drug-by-time model leaves no separately identifiable state coefficient. Do not claim that adding covariates magically solves that causal problem. Do not use the published all-sample selected-gene list as a leakage-free prediction feature set. The reported 65 genes at the authors' **q ≤ 0.1** are a reproduction reference, not an cognitive enhancement or new discovery threshold.

**Fail or continue.** Fail the shared-signature lead for this run if it only separates seen treatments, cannot track the withheld opening/closing contrast, or loses its interpretation under the identifiable nuisance comparisons. If metadata or design cannot distinguish explanations, record **inconclusive**, not a biological null. Continue to candidate interpretation if cross-treatment prediction and the temporal contrast support the same program without outcome leakage, with uncertainty and missing batch information exposed. That warrants a mechanism dossier and an independent-data search, not a causal or human enhancement claim.

**Output.** A sample/provenance table, held-out predictions, gene-rank stability results and a short verdict separating reproduced facts, new associations and unidentified causal questions. Save the analysis state and advance to Route 2 when this question is answered or blocked; do not keep changing splits until it works.

### Next experiment: an annotation-resistant dark-gene dossier

**Inputs.** GCST006572's `GWAS_CP_all.txt`; source genome-build and sample documentation; a compatible public LD reference and gene annotation; current Unknome protein/orthologue summary tables; Pharos `pharos400_tdls_canonicals.csv`; HPA adult expression. Use the source versions and terms. The GWAS FTP index lists the uncompressed file at 573M; it can be streamed. Leave SSGAC credentials, institutional cohorts, requester-pays burden data and full single-cell atlases out of the initial dependency chain.

**Steps.** Harmonize identifiers, alleles and genome build, and produce a gene-association inventory without using knownness to choose genomic evidence. Use published locus definitions or a documented standard association procedure; preserve uncertainty in locus-to-gene assignment. Join knownness as a separate axis, retaining continuous scores and unscored genes rather than declaring them known or irrelevant. Inspect gene-length, SNP-density, expression and annotation coverage effects using matched comparisons.

For the prioritized loci, compare competing genes under LD-aware evidence and available adult brain QTLs. Report shared-signal evidence and sensitivity to colocalization assumptions, not “expression causes intelligence.” Adult expression locates a candidate but does not establish adult causal timing. Check whether existing perturbation data cover the gene; a K562 phenotype is not a neuronal phenotype. Finally compare with Savage as overlapping evidence unless cohort independence is demonstrated. Separate cognitive-performance association from educational-attainment proxies and their indirect-effect issues [S17–S19].

**Fail or continue.** Fail this shortlist if every apparently unexplained hit depends on nearest-gene assignment, annotation artifacts, one inconsistent identifier mapping or treating reused data as independent confirmation. A locus that cannot distinguish neighboring genes stays unresolved, rather than being awarded to the least-studied one. Pursue candidates whose locus assignment remains credible under alternatives and whose adult biological context yields a falsifiable functional question. No arbitrary count of convergent scores, gene-percentile cutoff or required effect size defines success. A dossier still does not establish enhancement, a beneficial manipulation direction or acute reversibility.

**Output.** A ranked evidence table with locus alternatives, knownness provenance, developmental-versus-adult interpretations, data dependencies and specific disconfirming observations. A failure teaches where the public genetics loses resolution; proceed to Route 3 rather than enlarging a weak list indefinitely.

### Third experiment: a fixed-budget, switchable allocation benchmark

**Inputs.** Public e-prop/continual-learning methods and newly generated, fully published task instances for delayed learning, interference-sensitive recall, relational graph inference and distractor-resistant attention. Separate model-development structures from held-out structures; an unfamiliar graph with an identical memorized solution template is not strong transfer.

**Steps.** Construct a small recurrent or modular learner with a bounded memory and a controller that selects which eligible trace, memory or processing step receives an update or computation. The controller can see current observations and past feedback, not future labels or correct hidden task identities. Compare it with a tuned fixed policy, uniform extra gain and matched random routing. Count controller parameters, stored traces, retrieval, replay, operations and response deadlines in the same budget. Give every arm the same observations and equal opportunities to tune its settings.

Measure acquisition curves, delayed recall, old-task loss, reasoning accuracy and attention errors separately. Compare joint frontiers rather than a composite in which better recall conceals worse attention. Test switching the controller on and off on new material without resetting learned weights. Include task-rule changes and misleading feedback, and perform independent seeded runs. The benchmark specification and resource accounting are the experiment, not an assumed model-to-human conversion.

**Fail or continue.** Fail this candidate controller if its advantage disappears against the best resource-matched baseline, merely shifts the trade-off, requires privileged information or depends on added capacity. Wider uncertainty is inconclusive, not proof of equivalence. Pursue a reproducible cross-task frontier improvement whose mechanism survives ablation and whose operating policy can be reversed without deleting newly learned content. Report the actual task gains; do not set a made-up percentage improvement and call it cognitive enhancement. The next step would be finding a corresponding observable biological control variable, not promising the model's result in people.

**Output.** Reproducible generators, resource ledger, complete domain results and ablations, including negative transfer. Then advance to the remaining routes rather than polishing a single favorable benchmark.

## The largest unresolved question

**Does a healthy adult possess an acutely accessible control variable that improves several cognitive capacities together, rather than reallocating performance, repairing a deficit or exploiting a learned specialization—and can that state be switched back without compensating harm?**

The missing link is the conjunction of computational reserve and biological access. Animal gains show modifiability. Lifelong genetics shows associations. Plasticity signatures show changed conditions for learning. Human extremes show exceptional specializations. Closed-loop studies show selected state dependence. None establishes that shared, controllable reserve.

What would answer it is independent, controlled human evidence showing substantial useful gains in unfamiliar learning, later memory, reasoning and attention within the same rested healthy participants, with raw task outcomes, comparable opportunity and time, practice/expectancy controls and no hidden reciprocal losses. New-material testing after the state ends would distinguish reversible capacity from retained knowledge; physiological and functional follow-up would address unwanted persistence. A biological measurement would need to track and causally connect the proposed control variable, not merely accompany good scores. No fixed standardized-effect threshold can supply those missing facts.

Rolf's computation can identify a sharply testable lever, expose a false one, or show which data are insufficient. Confirming the human conjunction requires research access beyond this dry lab. Conversely, negative results on all six routes would reject particular tested hypotheses or expose unresolved gaps—not exhaust those research areas or prove that large-scale cognition is biologically impossible.

(2023), [critical-period reopening](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10284704/), DOI 10.1038/s41586-023-06204-3.** Confirmed adult-mouse social-reward endpoint, open/closed condition definitions, ECM-related transcription, the reported 65-gene result, batch adjustment and explicit omission of time. Confirmed GSE230679 and public-code statements. This supports a specific plasticity program, not broad human learning or acute full reversibility.
- **[S2] F — Park et al. (2023), [klotho and platelet factors](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10501899/), PMID 37587231.** Confirmed young-mouse effects, four-hour plasma sampling, preserved klotho effects in PF4-knockout mice and the authors' sufficient-but-not-necessary conclusion. Full text acknowledges nonspecific actions of the platelet-inhibition intervention and possible knockout compensation. Confirmed PXD040167/GSE171929 availability statements; necessity of a particular relay remains unknown.
- **[S3] F — Castner et al. (2023), [aged nonhuman primates](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10432271/), PMID 37400721.** Confirmed four-hour and 14–23-day observations, 18 monkeys overall but nine in the principal effective condition, no random allocation and blinding for only the majority of sessions. Did not extract graph-derived behavioral magnitude. The study cannot be called 18 independent responders or young-baseline enhancement.
- **[S4] F — Porrino et al. (2005), [CX717](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1188239/), PMID 16104830.** Confirmed the quoted normal-alert accuracy means and SEMs in the results, including the harder trials. This is genuine above-alert-baseline evidence, confined to the tested task and animals.
- **[S5] A — Johnson and Kang (2016), [ISRIB negative study](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27781164).** Confirmed lack of significant spatial-learning/memory enhancement in nontransgenic mice as well as lack of J20 rescue, and the stated target-engagement uncertainty. This is not solely a disease-model null.
- **[S6] A — Berg et al. (2021), [IGF-II/Angelman study](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34526125).** Confirmed that acute systemic IGF-II did not profoundly improve the assessed behavioral deficits or cognition; limited electrophysiological/motor effects were retained. The healthy-protocol replication detail is inherited from the animal report's full-text reading, not independently full-text-verified here.
- **[S7] A — Di Prisco et al. (2014), [eIF2α and object-place learning](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24974795).** Confirmed that deficient phospho-eIF2α-mediated translation impairs object-place learning despite that pathway's other enhancement associations. My XML request failed; the specifically pharmacological ISRIB attribution remains based on the animal report's full-text inspection, not my abstract check.
- **[S8] A — Roberts et al. (2020), [healthy acute pharmaceuticals](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/32709551).** Confirmed non-sleep-deprived healthy population and every SMD quoted above. Domain averages do not establish a common broad response in the same individuals.
- **[S9] A — Hu et al. (2020), [TMR meta-analysis](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/32027149).** Confirmed 91 experiments, 2,004 participants, g = 0.29 and its interval, with significant NREM rather than REM/waking effects in that analysis. This is memory consolidation evidence.
- **[S10] A — [Stanyer retraction notice](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40519131) (2025).** Confirmed incorrect sample sizes and use of SEM instead of SD, and the explicit invalidation of results/conclusions. No invalidated efficacy estimate is used here.
- **[S11] A — Shaw et al. (2023), [acute normobaric hyperoxia](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/37121344).** Confirmed all quoted domain estimates, 23 studies/76 effect estimates, low evidence quality and prediction intervals often crossing zero. Full text and numerical extraction dataset were not available through the opened record. The large estimates are reported, not independently reconstructed.
- **[S12] A — Zhu et al. (2025), [photobiomodulation meta-analysis](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40394373).** Confirmed 24 trials/820 participants, the quoted SMDs/intervals, heterogeneous populations and the nonsignificant healthy executive-reaction-time subgroup. Did not infer an unreported healthy-only broad effect or single acute protocol.
- **[S13] A — Keefe et al. (2025), [CONNEX](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41233083).** Confirmed the phase 3 null, 1,835 treated participants and the quoted MCCB contrast/interval. It is strong evidence against the tested CIAS program, not a universal NMDA or healthy-enhancement impossibility result.
- **[S14] A — Horan et al. (2025), [xanomeline/trospium cognition](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39659157).** Confirmed prespecified impaired-subgroup d = 0.54, stricter impairment subgroup d = 0.80, no significant full-sample effect and five-week clinical context. This preserves the positive finding without exporting it to healthy acute cognition.
- **[S15] A — LePort et al. (2012), [HSAM](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/22652113).** Confirmed the autobiographical/laboratory-memory dissociation in eleven participants. Exceptional memory is not uniformly superior laboratory cognition.
- **[S16] F — Bellec et al. (2020), [e-prop](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7367848/).** Confirmed its computational credit-assignment claim and the full-text statement that learning is slower than BPTT while approximating its performance. Open reference code does not constitute human enhancement evidence.
- **[S17] A — Savage et al. (2018), [intelligence GWAS](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29942086).** Confirmed 269,867 participants, 205 loci, 1,016 mapped/associated genes and the reported neuronal enrichments. These are not 1,016 confirmed causal enhancement targets.
- **[S18] A — Okbay et al. (2022), [within-/between-family educational attainment](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/35361970).** Confirmed that direct effects explain roughly half the polygenic index's association magnitude in the reported analysis and the assortment concern. This is not a numerical correction to apply to every cognition GWAS.
- **[S19] A — Rocha et al. (2023), [Unknome](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/37552676).** Confirmed the unknown-gene resource and experimental functional screening. It supports the discovery strategy, not a cognitive role for every low-knownness gene.
- **[S20] F — Bowman et al. (2023), [effort versus quality](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10266726/).** Confirmed randomized crossover context, knapsack/CANTAB measurements and public code/data statement. Opened PECO table headers showing participant, session and treatment identifiers. No reanalysis performed; more effort must not be substituted for better solutions.
- **[S21] A — Rudoler et al. (2024), [noninvasive memory timing](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39033965).** An additional primary source reached through the public-data portal. Confirmed a larger good-minus-poor recall contrast with better out-of-sample decoding, not universal benefit or improvement over every ordinary-presentation comparator. Opened the linked dataset description/README independently.
- **[S22] A — Hedge et al. (2018), [reliability paradox](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28726177).** Confirmed the distinction between robust experimental effects and reliable individual differences. This qualifies responder searches, not the ability of cognitive science to detect a large group effect.
