# The body's cognitive control mechanisms: where could an enhancement-like lever hide?

## Bottom line

**No mechanism reviewed establishes the full conjunction: minutes-to-hours onset, a reversible enhanced state, large gains, breadth across learning, memory, reasoning and attention, in healthy people.** That is an evidence verdict, not a biological impossibility proof. There is no validated numeric definition of “large” for cognitive enhancement; none is invented here.

The useful distinction is **control of a particular computation versus amplification of a shared signal**. A target name alone does not specify which cell, compartment, phase of learning or downstream effector is affected. Hippocampal plasticity can benefit from cAMP, whereas excessive cAMP opens channels that weaken prefrontal working-memory networks. Even perfect subtype selectivity is not necessarily circuit or compartment selectivity.

There are therefore **two discovery axes: target × approach** (§§12–15). Allostery, state dependence, biased signaling and interaction-specific modulation can change what a familiar target does without changing its name. Conversely, an unfamiliar target is not a novel approach. The target map separates healthy enhancement, deficit rescue, genetic necessity and unlocated evidence; no row is certified “never tried.” The best provisional leads below are mostly **untested target–approach pairs**, not genes with no cognition literature.

My strongest molecular leads are compartment-aware cyclic-nucleotide control and activity-dependent glutamatergic modulation. My strongest *profile-shaped hypothesis* is coordinated, state-dependent control of encoding, persistent firing and interference protection—not a global plasticity increase. Peripheral klotho-related signaling is an important less-resolved lead because acute above-control effects occur in young mice without klotho itself entering the brain. None establishes large broad healthy-human enhancement or complete state reversal.

### Evidence and verification conventions

Targeted narrative research checked on **5 October 2026**, not a systematic literature census. Category coverage spans all requested pathway and approach classes; gene-by-gene target coverage, literature searches and replication coverage are **partial**, not a census of the druggable proteome. Several full texts, supplements, individual outcomes and exact graph-derived magnitudes were **unavailable or not extracted**, specified below. No intervention, reanalysis, simulation or Modal job was run.

Every numbered citation was resolved through Europe PMC's REST API and its indexed abstract inspected. **A** in the final table means **abstract-only**; **F** additionally means relevant full-text results/mechanism/method passages were inspected. F does not mean independent reproduction or complete risk-of-bias appraisal. Failed XML retrievals were not counted as full-text access; PMC HTML supplied the successful fallbacks identified in the table. Historical reviews support attributed mechanisms, not an exhaustive current efficacy claim.

For each pathway, “best evidence” means the strongest relevant **located** above-control finding, not a proven winner across all studies. **NR** means the inspected material did not provide a usable numerical cognitive magnitude, or graph values were not extracted. NR never means zero. Molecular concentration, receptor occupancy, spine count and LTP amplitude are not cognitive effect sizes. Standardized effects, raw task scores and retention durations cannot be added or converted to percentage intelligence.

“Reversible” means the induced capacity/state and unwanted effects return to baseline. Retained knowledge need not disappear. Clearance, ending stimulation, or a nonsignificant late comparison alone does not establish complete reversal. Healthy older participants are not automatically impaired; improvement above age-matched controls is enhancement in that population, not proof of exceeding a young baseline.

## 1. An organizing map: what has to work together

A useful schematic—not a quantitatively validated model—is:

> peripheral/circadian/metabolic state → neuromodulatory allocation → receptor/channel-dependent processing → synapse-specific modification → protein-dependent stabilization → replay/integration → useful retrieval and reasoning

These are coupled loops, not independent knobs. Astrocytes, blood vessels and immune cells contribute throughout. Homeostatic plasticity changes receptor trafficking and excitation/inhibition to stabilize activity after perturbation [44]. The compensation exists; its size and timing for a hypothetical enhancer are not known.

| Cognitive operation | Bodily machinery principally implicated here | What “turning it up” misses |
|---|---|---|
| Attend and select | Noradrenergic/cholinergic gain, inhibition, cortical timing | More gain can privilege distractors or narrow selection. |
| Hold information | Recurrent excitation, NMDA currents, HCN/KCNQ and other intrinsic currents | More plasticity is not more stable persistent firing. |
| Learn | Coincidence detection, neuromodulatory teaching signals, synaptic tags | Stronger updates cannot supply missing feedback or assign credit correctly. |
| Retain/integrate | CREB, translation, BDNF, glial support, sleep coupling | Stabilizing everything can preserve errors and increase interference. |
| Switch/reason | Flexible network recruitment, stable representations, controlled retrieval | Stability and flexibility can demand opposite settings; no molecular “reasoning multiplier” is established. |

The negative in the last row is important: most molecular enhancement studies measure recognition, fear learning or spatial behavior, not unfamiliar multistep reasoning.

## 2. Second messengers and their breakdown

### 2.1 cAMP → PKA/CREB and signal breakdown

**Controls.** Adenylyl cyclases generate cAMP; PDEs degrade it. cAMP can activate PKA, with downstream phosphorylation and CREB-dependent transcription supporting durable synaptic changes [1,2]. It also directly gates HCN channels: its cognitive effects are not all mediated through CREB [6]. Spatial and temporal signaling patterns matter as much as total concentration.

**Healthy above-baseline evidence.** Acute roflumilast, a PDE4-family inhibitor, improved immediate verbal recall in healthy young adults by **2–3 words on a 30-word task**; administration preceded testing by one hour. This was an acute randomized blinded crossover proof of concept, not demonstrated broad enhancement [3, A]. In **20 healthy adults aged 60–80**, screened for normal memory, another crossover found delayed verbal-recall **Cohen's d = 0.69**, but no spatial-memory effect [4, A]. Neither result identifies the responsible subtype or establishes complete recovery of an enhancement state.

**Plateau/backfire.** PDE4 inhibition can cause nausea/emesis; an animal tolerability surrogate is not human safety proof [2–4]. PKA can increase PDE4 activity/expression, providing feedback [2]. Breakdown also shapes information-bearing signal patterns, so removing it is not equivalent to improving cognition [1,2]. An allosteric inhibitor study found acute behavior/cAMP effects without detectable one-hour CREB-phosphorylation or BDNF elevation; those markers rose after repeated treatment [2, F]. Do not compress this into “acute CREB-driven brain rebuilding.” The approach axis below asks whether regulatory-site, state- or compartment-selective control could preserve useful signaling rather than indiscriminately raise it.

### 2.2 The crucial counter-direction: prefrontal cAMP–HCN/KCNQ

**Controls.** Primate dorsolateral prefrontal recurrent networks sustain spatially tuned delay firing. Postsynaptic α2A-adrenoceptor stimulation inhibits cAMP, closes HCN channels and strengthens connectivity; excessive cAMP collapses delay-related firing [6, A]. In aged monkeys, cAMP inhibition or HCN/KCNQ blockade partially restores delay firing [7, A]. This is physiology/age-related restoration, not a broad healthy-human effect.

**Above baseline, onset, reversal.** The Wang study also reports better working-memory behavior after PFC HCN1 blockade or knockdown; numerical behavioral size and full behavioral recovery are NR in the abstract [6]. Pharmacological/electrophysiological modulation is rapid, whereas gene knockdown is not an acute intervention. The study must not be generalized to all cortical areas, ages or HCN isoforms.

**Why more can hurt.** A sustained working-memory representation needs appropriately strong recurrent coupling, not continually updated connections or indiscriminate firing. The contrast with hippocampal cAMP-dependent consolidation is a demonstrated mechanism conflict, not merely a speculative safety caveat. Conversely, globally suppressing cAMP to protect PFC could damage learning elsewhere. The wanted lever would have to distinguish region, compartment and task phase.

### 2.3 PKA/CREB beyond PDEs

**Controls/evidence.** CREB helps convert activity into lasting gene-expression programs and changes neuronal excitability. Constitutively active CREB in behaving transgenic mice enhanced hippocampal LTP and transiently accelerated eyeblink acquisition, but sustained chronic activation **impaired the same task** [8, A; magnitude NR]. This is genetic, not an hours-scale reversible human intervention. The PDE4 human effects are indirect evidence for the upstream pathway, not proof that specifically activating CREB caused their gains.

**Limits.** More excitability can change which neurons win memory allocation rather than raise general capacity. Transcription/protein changes and acquired memories outlast the initiating signal. Appropriate activation time and synaptic specificity remain unresolved; a “memory gene” signature does not establish reasoning enhancement.

### 2.4 cGMP → PKG, and the other PDE families

**Controls.** Nitric oxide–soluble guanylyl cyclase signaling generates cGMP, which can act through PKG in synaptic plasticity and through vascular pathways [1]. Cyclic-nucleotide pools are not interchangeable. A review's early-versus-late consolidation account is a useful rodent hypothesis, not a universal human schedule.

A healthy-volunteer PDE9A study provides a particularly clean separation: **20 healthy men**, CSF cGMP peak **2–5 hours**, return toward baseline **10–14 hours** [9, A]. This establishes reversible **biomarker** modulation, not better cognition. Subsequent trials in **457 prodromal/mild Alzheimer participants** found no cognitive benefit [10, A]. Failure in patients does not directly test healthy enhancement, but defeats the claim that raising cGMP alone guarantees useful cognition.

## 3. Glutamate receptors, inhibitory tone and channels

### 3.1 Glutamate: coincidence, transmission and selective weakening

|---|---|---|---|
| **NMDA receptors; GluN2B, co-agonist availability** | Activity-dependent coincidence detection and Ca²⁺ entry initiate plasticity; recurrent NMDA-mediated excitation also supports holding representations. | Forebrain GluN2B overexpression improved several learning/memory tasks in intact mice [11, A; size NR]. Constitutive genetic alteration: no acute onset/reversal. Ca²⁺/calmodulin, CaMKII, MAPK/ERK and IP₃/DAG connect receptor activation to trafficking, transcription and translation [1,32]. They are convergence machinery, not independently established broad healthy enhancers. Increased kinase activation or LTP cannot substitute for behavioral transfer. On the phosphatase side, genetic inhibition of PP1 improved learning with closely spaced training and prolonged memory in mice [76, A; size NR]. That establishes a tested brake, not an acutely reversible drug effect or a beneficial direction for every phosphatase. PPM1E can bind postsynaptic density and dephosphorylate CaMKII in biochemical assays [77, A]; this makes an interaction-specific hypothesis concrete, not behaviorally validated. This map is strongest for NMDA/AMPA and group I mGluR examples; other metabotropic subtypes, receptor trafficking and subcellular signaling are less comprehensively surveyed.

### 3.2 GABA is not simply the enemy of enhancement

**Subunit/cell-type map.** GABA-A receptors are heteromeric chloride channels. Reviewed genetic pharmacology separates α1-associated benzodiazepine sedation/amnesia from α2-associated anxiolysis, with α3 roles and α5-enriched hippocampal inhibition. α4/α6-containing receptors do not bind clinically used classical benzodiazepines; extrasynaptic receptors additionally contribute tonic inhibition [16, F; 17, A]. These are functional associations, not a claim that each subunit has only one job. GABA-B supplies slower metabotropic inhibition; it is not the source of the α-subunit findings.

**Best healthy evidence.** α5 deletion improved spatial water-maze learning in mice, without changing the reported non-hippocampal learning/anxiety tasks [15, A; magnitude NR]. It is developmental/constitutive, not acute or reversible. The full review also describes pharmacological α5-selective negative modulation improving rodent water-maze performance; its healthy-human example reversed alcohol impairment, **not** intact-baseline enhancement [16]. Numerical size/onset/full recovery of an above-baseline human effect are not established here.

A complementary result is more revealing than “remove inhibition”: activating **CCK-expressing GABA neurons** improved social/object recognition, contextual learning/discrimination and puzzle-box problem solving in intact mice [18, A; magnitudes NR]. Genetic/chemogenetic preparation preceded acute activation. The abstract reports a small anxiety increase in one assay, not zero emotional effects. Stopping activation does not reverse the prerequisite genetic intervention or prove complete physiological recovery.

**Tradeoffs.** Inhibition controls competition, timing and signal discrimination as well as suppressing activity. Broad negative GABA-A modulation risks anxiety/convulsant effects; subtype-selective compounds can avoid some of those animal effects yet still fail human tolerability [16]. α5 suppression can also harm selected functions, and both positive and negative α5 modulation have benefited different impaired models [17]. Neither tonic disinhibition nor stronger interneuron firing is a universal direction. α1–3/α4–δ/GABA-B have no large broad acute healthy above-baseline gain established in this pass; their cognitive role and absence of qualifying evidence must not be conflated.

### 3.3 Intrinsic channels and persistent firing

- **HCN and KCNQ:** control dendritic coupling, excitability and the persistence of representations. The PFC results in §2.2 are the strongest direct evidence here: HCN1 manipulation improved working-memory behavior; HCN/KCNQ modulation restored aged-monkey delay firing [6,7]. Behavioral size/full recovery NR. These channels also perform useful functions elsewhere, so global blockade cannot be assumed to preserve healthy processing.
- **Ca²⁺-activated K⁺/SK channels:** regulate afterhyperpolarization and provide negative feedback on NMDA signaling, constraining excitability and plasticity [21, A]. The review establishes the mechanism; this pass does not establish a quantified broad healthy enhancement through SK modulation. Acute conductance changes are possible, but a cognitive state and its reversal are not thereby demonstrated. Removing feedback can destabilize processing; activating it too strongly can suppress needed responses.
- **Muscarinic-dependent Ca²⁺-sensitive nonselective cation current:** entorhinal layer-V neurons can maintain graded firing after a stimulus, robust to distractors in slice recordings [19, A]. This is a cellular substrate, not a demonstrated behavioral gain. The initially attractive attribution to TRPC channels was contradicted by mice lacking relevant TRPC combinations or all seven TRPC family members, which retained persistent activity [20, A]. Molecular identity remains unresolved in this evidence. There is no reported human magnitude/onset/reversal to rank.

Persistent activity in a slice is not synonymous with a complete human working-memory system. No identified channel alone establishes expanded healthy capacity, more accurate reasoning and preserved flexibility.

## 5. Plasticity, protein synthesis and the cost of removing brakes

### 5.1 mTOR–4E-BP/S6K versus the integrated stress response

**mTOR controls translation availability**, including cap-dependent initiation through 4E-BPs and signaling through S6 kinases; its upstream inputs include activity and growth/metabolic signals [32, F]. This is required for some durable memory but does not mean “more protein synthesis = more intelligence.” No selective mTOR increase with an established large acute healthy above-baseline cognitive gain was located. Magnitude/onset/state reversal of that claim are unavailable.

A direct counterexample is **Tsc2-heterozygous mice**: hyperactive mTOR lowered the LTP threshold yet impaired hippocampal learning; rapamycin rescued the deficit [33, A]. That is pathology rescue, not healthy enhancement. It shows why a larger plasticity readout can accompany worse learning. Altered protein production, cellular growth and homeostatic responses need not end with drug clearance.

**ISR/eIF2α controls which messages get translated under stress and learning.** Phosphorylated eIF2α reduces general translation while permitting selected transcripts, including ATF4; the ISR is an adaptive response, not merely wasteful suppression [32,34]. ISRIB counteracts the translational consequences of phosphorylation. The original study found in intact mice, using **weak training**, water-maze escape latency after five days **16.4 ± 4.8 s versus 68.1 ± 20 s** (reported means ± SEM; **eight mice per arm**) [34, F]. Treatment followed each trial; difference developed across training days. A single post-training treatment improved **24-hour contextual fear memory**, not one-hour memory. Thus an acute molecular input can alter later consolidation without immediately creating an enhanced waking cognitive state.

**Backfire/replication:** an independent study found no significant spatial-memory enhancement in nontransgenic mice [35, A]. More decisively, ISRIB before learning impaired a different intact-mouse **object-place** task, consistent with loss of mGluR-LTD, without a locomotor explanation [36, F]. It was not solely a lifelong-mutant effect. Blocking an adaptive stress response also increased vulnerability of stressed cells in the original cell experiments [34]; that is a mechanistic liability, not proof of that harm in healthy humans. Full enhanced-state reversal and broad human efficacy are unestablished. A faster acquisition/retention result must be evaluated alongside LTD-dependent learning, updating and old-memory preservation.

### 5.2 BDNF/TrkB

**Controls.** Activity-induced BDNF/TrkB signaling helps stabilize synaptic changes and interacts with protein synthesis and synaptic tagging. The inspected review explicitly leaves roles in acquisition, retention and extinction, and spatial specificity, unresolved [37, A].

**Healthy evidence.** BDNF-associated LTP/plasticity is much better established than a selective above-baseline human cognition gain. Moliner reports psychedelic binding to TrkB and BDNF-dependent plasticity/antidepressant-like behavior in mice, separable from 5-HT2A-mediated head twitch [38, A]. That is not a demonstrated gain in healthy learning, memory, reasoning and attention; binding affinity is not cognitive magnitude. No direct broad healthy gain size/onset/reversal is established here. BDNF increases after repeated second-messenger modulation likewise cannot identify BDNF as the mediator of an acute gain [2].

**Plateau.** More synapses or stronger trophic signaling do not specify which information should be stored. Plasticity can outlast the acute input; separating trophic change, subjective state and useful learning requires distinct evidence. For acute impairment and human post-acute gaps, retain the companion [acute report, §1](acute-mechanisms.md#1-rapid-plasticity), rather than inheriting an animal marker as human enhancement.

### 5.3 Epigenetic brakes

**Controls.** Histone acetylation/deacetylation regulates transcriptional accessibility. Focal HDAC3 deletion or inhibition enhanced persistent long-term memory in intact mice; Nr4a2 knockdown abolished the deletion-associated gain [39, A; behavioral size NR]. This is useful causal evidence for a transcriptional brake, but focal gene deletion is not acute/reversible and pharmacological state recovery was not established.

**Human above baseline.** Valproate improved trained pitch-category identification in adult men, without a general cognitive change [40, F]. **24 enrolled, 18 completed both crossover arms**; the strongest finding was in the first treatment arm, and the second arm failed to reproduce that advantage, with interference/carryover a concern. Treatment was a **15-day course**, with training during the course—not minutes-to-hours acquisition of absolute pitch. It was association of a limited set of pitch classes with labels, not proof of natural expert-level absolute pitch. A numerical standardized cognitive gain was not extracted here.

**Limits.** Valproate has multiple actions; this trial does not isolate HDAC inhibition as the human cause. Opening transcriptional access can affect many programs, and learning/interference persists across periods. Broad cognitive gain and full reversal are unestablished. Replication matters more than the phrase “critical-period reopening.”

### 5.4 Perineuronal nets and extracellular matrix

**Controls.** Nets around selected neurons constrain experience-driven synaptic remodeling and contribute to mature circuit organization. Enzymatic digestion reopened ocular-dominance plasticity in adult rats [42, A]; that is a sensory plasticity readout, not general cognitive enhancement.

There is also genuine **intact-adult cognitive enhancement**: local perirhinal chondroitinase treatment in mice supported object recognition at **24 and 48 hours**, where control animals no longer recognized at 24 hours [41, F]. Testing started **one week after treatment**, not acutely. Genetic net attenuation showed a similar phenotype. In a small repeated cohort, enhancement declined and treated/control performance became similar at **6–8 weeks**, alongside reappearing nets. That supports recovery of this specific learning phenotype, **not complete whole-brain physiological equivalence** or a fast on/off window.

**Limits.** Nets are not just redundant walls. Loosening constraints changes stability and circuit specificity; new learning may overwrite useful organization. A recent direct regional neurocan depletion study found temporal-order/reversal-learning impairments [75, A]. This is a different ECM manipulation, not a refutation of the perirhinal result; it undercuts a global “less matrix is always better” claim. Psychedelic-reopened social reward learning in adult mice involves ECM-related programs and restored oxytocin-dependent LTD [43, A], but is narrow and can persist days/weeks. Structural modification fails the requested reversible state shape even if some behavioral effects eventually recover.

This is **necessity/rescue**, not a quantified healthy above-baseline gain. Cognitive-gain onset/reversal unestablished. | Rescue does not show excess lactate expands capacity. Fuel/signaling versus activity-dependent allocation remain difficult to distinguish. |
| **Astrocytic Gq signaling** | Can coordinate task-associated potentiation and memory allocation. | Designer-receptor/optical activation during learning enhanced next-day recall in intact mice; LTP persisted after activation stopped [48, A; magnitude NR]. | The acute input is not an acutely reversible induced state; genetic preparation is not transient. Direct neuronal excitation instead impaired memory, demonstrating that useful allocation differs from more activity. |
| **Oligodendrocytes/myelin** | Supports reliable transmission and learning-related circuit adaptation. | Blocking new adult oligodendrocytes, while sparing existing myelin, prevented mice from mastering a complex wheel [49, A]. This is a necessity result, not proof of above-baseline cognitive enhancement by adding myelin. | No quantified acute reversible healthy gain established. Structural remodeling/training takes time; more or faster conduction alone need not improve cross-circuit timing. |

The energetic constraint is real, but these sources do not measure a universal healthy-human reserve or prove that the brain is already globally optimal. Efficient routing could, in principle, help without more total ATP; that possibility is developed computationally in the theory report, not demonstrated biologically here.

## 7. Signals from the rest of the body

### 7.1 Exercise-related muscle, liver and circulating factors

**Controls.** Exercise changes arousal, vasculature, metabolism and secreted factors. The mechanisms are coupled, so an acute exercise effect does not identify a single circulating mediator.

- **Cathepsin B:** running increased systemic levels; knockout eliminated running-associated neurogenesis/spatial-memory benefit in mice. Human changes correlated with fitness and hippocampal memory [58, A]. This is causal animal mediation plus human association, not administration-induced healthy-human enhancement. Numerical cognitive magnitude and acute factor-specific onset/reversal NR.
- **FNDC5/irisin:** knockout impaired exercise-related cognition; local replacement rescued knockout pattern separation; peripheral overexpression improved AD-model deficits [57, A]. These are not a direct large broad above-intact-baseline human gain. Neurogenesis and viral overexpression fail an acute reversible intervention profile. Size/onset/reversal of the requested effect are unestablished.
- **Liver-derived GPLD1:** exercised-aged-mouse plasma transferred selected benefits; raising circulating GPLD1 ameliorated age-related deficits. Higher levels in active healthy older humans were observational [56, A]. This is aged-animal restoration, not proven intact-young enhancement, and not an established hours-scale switch. Direct cognitive magnitude/full recovery NR.

These findings establish an interesting body–brain communication problem, not an “exercise molecule = cognitive enhancement” chain. Redundancy, age dependence and multiple downstream effects can limit specificity. For the small directly measured acute exercise effects and the disagreement among chronic meta-analyses, use the companion [non-drug report, §8](nondrug-interventions.md#8-exercise-and-its-molecular-mediators).

### 7.2 Klotho, platelets/PF4 and other blood-borne factors

**Controls/evidence.** Peripheral klotho enhanced Y-maze behavior in **young intact mice within four hours**, while klotho itself was not detected entering the brain in the cited evidence [53, F]. PF4 entered brain parenchyma and enhanced young-mouse spatial learning/memory with repeated daily treatment; acute receptor-dependent LTP is a separate physiological result. Young-water-maze groups were **13 controls/12 PF4**, with behavioral means largely graph-derived and not extracted here. Do not replace cognitive magnitude with the nearly doubled platelet activation.

The strongest species bridge is aged rhesus monkeys: cognition improved at **four hours** and remained detectable at **14–23 days** after a single klotho treatment [54, F]. There were **18 monkeys overall**, but **nine** in the principal effective condition; sessions are not independent animals. Allocation was not randomized, and only a majority of sessions were blinded. Graph-derived behavioral size was not extracted. This is enhancement above an aged comparator, not a tested healthy-young ceiling, and durable benefit is not evidence of rapid enhanced-state reversal.

**Where the mechanism runs out.** Klotho still enhanced memory in **PF4-knockout mice** [53]. PF4 is sufficient to reproduce selected effects but is not established as the necessary relay. Other factors, redundancy or knockout compensation remain live possibilities. GluN2B dependence of PF4-related LTP identifies downstream machinery, not all steps from blood to behavior. The receptors/transport/relay identity and beneficial-versus-deleterious platelet activation remain incompletely mapped.

**Other blood factors.** Young plasma improved aged-mouse contextual/spatial memory with CREB-associated changes [55, A]. This is restoration with a complex mixture, not acute healthy enhancement, and neither exact beneficial composition nor whole-state reversal is established. Treating every “young blood” change as desirable is unjustified. Klotho/PF4/GPLD1 and brain plasticity mechanisms overlap; they are not independent increments that can be added.

### 7.3 Growth factors, thyroid and stress hormones

- **IGF-II:** post-training or post-retrieval hippocampal administration enhanced retention/prevented forgetting in intact rats, requiring IGF-II receptors, protein synthesis and a sensitive consolidation window [59, A]. Cognitive size NR; acute input, delayed retention outcome; full state reversal unestablished. It is not new knowledge or broad reasoning. A systemic Angelman-model study was negative on cognition [60, A], qualifying generalization across context/route rather than negating local intact-rat enhancement.
- **Thyroid:** maintains metabolic/neuropsychiatric function. Overt hypothyroidism produces deficits often improved by replacement [61, A]. No above-baseline enhancer in euthyroid healthy people is established here. A **94-person** older subclinical-hypothyroidism trial was cognitively null at **six/twelve months** [62, A]. Those times and that population do not test acute healthy enhancement. Biochemical normalization/clearance and full neuropsychiatric recovery differ.
- **Stress hormones:** act with noradrenergic and immune signals to change encoding, storage and retrieval. Meta-analysis of **113 human studies/6,216 people** found post-encoding stress could improve later memory, whereas stress near retrieval impaired it and encoding effects depended on context/timing [63, A; no single meaningful broad effect size]. These acute selective gains are not broad healthy enhancement; cortisol response magnitude did not explain memory effects. State recovery was not comprehensively established. A stronger stress response can prioritize salient material at the expense of executive control and other memories.

The companion endocrine assessment also covers insulin, testosterone and growth hormone. Its clinical normalization/null evidence is not silently promoted here to a healthy-body optimization lever.

### 7.4 Inflammation and immune/glial signaling

**Controls.** Low-level immune signals participate in plasticity, consolidation and neurogenesis; strong infection/injury/stress-related responses can impair them. Cytokines, peripheral immune cells, microglia and astrocytes interact with neurotransmitters and glucocorticoids [64, A]. Inflammation can change an upstream bodily state and downstream neural learning rules together.

**Evidence boundary.** Reducing excessive inflammation can restore function, but a large broad acute gain from suppressing normal immune signaling in healthy people is not established here. Magnitude/onset/reversal of that claim are unavailable. Suppressing all cytokine activity is not the logical consequence of inflammation-associated impairment: physiological signaling has useful functions. Homeostatic glial signaling, protective responses and harmful chronic activation must be separated [44,64]. The review supports the dual role; this pass did not exhaustively map microglial subtypes or blood–brain immune transport.

## 8. Ranking against the *whole* cognitive enhancement profile

**Established:** replicated/meta-analytic evidence for the specified limited outcome/population. **Supported:** controlled positive/mechanistic evidence, but limited replication, scope or conflicting findings. **Speculative:** missing direct evidence for the claimed coordinated/broad effect. These labels attach to claims, not entire pathways. No row earns “established cognitive enhancement.” This is a qualitative research ranking, not a probability, usage recommendation or common-scale efficacy comparison.

| Rank / mechanism | Actual evidence status | Acute onset and healthy baseline | Magnitude/breadth and reversal | What would have to be true to approach cognitive enhancement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Compartment-aware cAMP/PDE regulation while preserving PFC firing** | **Supported** narrow PDE4 healthy-human memory gain and PDE8B mouse genetic enhancement. Coordinated control **speculative**. | One-hour healthy-young PDE4 testing; older healthy verbal benefit. | 2–3 recalled words; older verbal d = 0.69, spatial null. Broad transfer and complete state recovery absent. | The favorable hippocampal computation must be reachable without excessive PFC cAMP or loss of updating; benefit must extend beyond verbal memory, with measured acute capacity/recovery. Subtype selection alone cannot establish this. |
| **2. Activity-dependent glutamate facilitation plus appropriately timed inhibition** | **Supported** substantial intact-alert-primate AMPA task gain and multi-task mouse CCK result; combination **speculative**. | Acute primate test-session effect; interneuron experiments require genetic preparation. | Large-looking raw task gains, not broad human cognition. Complete reversal unestablished. | Useful inputs must be preferentially amplified while competition/timing remain intact; human learning/reasoning must improve together without excitation-related costs or hidden task strategy. |
| **3. Adaptive neuromodulatory/circuit state control** | **Established** small selected acute healthy-human catecholamine/cholinergic effects. Adaptive broad controller **speculative**. | Acute input is plausible; learned/calibrated control can have long prerequisites. | Best-supported averages modest/selective; stability/flexibility tradeoffs. Reversible input ≠ demonstrated reversible state. | Healthy brains must have avoidable shared allocation inefficiency, not only low-baseline headroom, and observables must permit causal correction at fixed time/information budget. |
| **4. Peripheral klotho-related relay control** | **Supported** young-mouse enhancement and aged-monkey bridge; full relay and healthy-human action **speculative**. | Four-hour mouse/aged-monkey evidence, PF4 behavioral protocols often repeated. | Selected spatial/memory outcomes; magnitude not independently extracted; primate persistence to 14–23 days undermines a fast switch claim. | An adult relay must be identified, produce useful multi-domain gains in healthy humans, and permit safe state termination despite durable downstream changes. |
| **5. Oxygen/metabolic reserve modulation** | Hyperoxia's acute cross-domain signal **supported, low confidence**; ketone effects modest/mixed. Broad reserve **speculative**. | Acute healthy-human hyperoxia experiments exist. | Some g estimates 0.59–0.88, but low-quality heterogeneous studies; not a joint same-person broad effect. Full reversal missing. | Independent healthy-human evidence must retain magnitude across unfamiliar domains and show a genuinely limiting supply process—not alerting, measurement artifacts or compensating physiological effects. |
| **6. Selective translation/epigenetic/ECM plasticity with interference protection** | **Supported** animal learning changes, including contradictory/null results; broad coordinated benefit **speculative**. | Acute molecular inputs possible; demonstrated outcomes often next day/multi-day. PNN and pitch courses slower. | Strong selected animal memory effects, but task costs; structural/transcriptional persistence conflicts with rapid reversibility. | Right synapses/transcripts must change without impaired LTD, wrong-memory stabilization or old-knowledge loss; human post-acute learning rate, reasoning and recovery must be demonstrated. |

**Why sleep is not higher here:** NREM targeted reactivation has an established modest consolidation effect (**g = 0.29**) [66], and coordinated rhythms have causal animal support [67]. It strengthens prior material after sleep rather than yielding the required broad waking state. Its good evidence is preserved, not used to replace Rolf's question with “remember selected items better.”

## 9. Can different bottlenecks add up?

**Possibly—but the evidence does not justify predicting an cognitive enhancement stack.** Independent-looking molecules often converge on the same synaptic machinery. Different biological targets also do not establish statistically independent effects or complementary task bottlenecks.

| Pairing idea | Evidence actually available | What it supports / what remains missing |
|---|---|---|
| **Cholinergic encoding + PDE4-supported stabilization** | Roflumilast plus donepezil at individually ineffective conditions restored scopolamine-impaired rat recognition [69, A]. | A context-specific interaction/rescue, not additive healthy gains. The muscarinic insult favors this pairing. A healthy factorial test with both single arms, matched training and several domains is missing. |
| **Timing + plasticity** | In mice, inducing spindles **in phase** with slow-oscillation up-states improved consolidation, unlike out-of-phase induction [67, A]. | Causal evidence that coordination matters; not two pills summing, and not broad immediate reasoning. Timing can make the same input useful or ineffective. |
| **More consolidation across memory types** | Pharmacologically spindle-enriched naps improved verbal memory but **worsened perceptual learning**, leaving motor learning unchanged [68, A]. | Direct human above-sleep-baseline benefit with a reciprocal cost; consolidation is not a scalar reserve. |
| **Lifestyle levers affecting different bodily routes** | **585-person** exercise/mindfulness factorial trial found no significant cognitive benefit or interaction at its main endpoint [70, A]. | A direct counterexample to automatic synergy, in older adults with subjective concerns over months—not a universal acute combination null. |
| **AMPA facilitation + selective inhibition; PFC stability + hippocampal encoding; learning-rate increase + selective replay/protection** | Each component has selected mechanism evidence; a qualifying coordinated broad healthy-human result was not located. | **Speculative**. They could move a joint frontier outward, or cancel/redistribute gains. Computational tests can reject hidden-resource explanations but cannot establish biological access. |

A useful analysis needs the interaction and joint domain outcomes, not a composite driven by one measure. “A improves memory; B improves attention” across unrelated studies does not show simultaneous benefit in one healthy person. Neither raw percentages nor g/d values can be arithmetically summed. Sequential encoding/consolidation settings may be complementary without being an acute broad enhanced state.

## 10. Where the map runs out

1. **Addressable credit assignment.** We know many signals that permit an update; no known acute bodily lever delivers correct, high-dimensional feedback to all relevant synapses for arbitrary novel tasks. Increasing plasticity does not solve that informational problem. This is a missing lever, not a proved ceiling.
2. **Reasoning and flexible routing.** Many mechanisms alter recall or activity; molecular studies rarely assay novel multistep reasoning alongside learning and attention. Stable working memory, useful abstraction and flexible updating are not known to share a beneficial scalar control.
3. **Interference-free rapid integration.** Replay, LTD, inhibition and slow consolidation can protect structure. There is no known acute broadly effective switch that speeds unfamiliar learning while preserving old knowledge, calibration and flexibility. Their exact relation to accessible signaling pools is poorly mapped.
4. **Subcellular addressability.** Gene expression and subtype-selective binding do not reveal active cAMP nanodomains, channel localization, synaptic tags or local protein synthesis during real cognition. The wanted spatial/temporal control might exist, but bulk RNA and CSF measurements cannot demonstrate it.
5. **Peripheral relays.** Klotho/PF4, exercise factors, immune/endocrine signals can reach cognition through multiple routes. Which intermediates are necessary, which are compensatory, and whether favorable versus harmful systemic states can be separated are unresolved.
6. **Persistent-firing biophysics and glia.** TRPC-independent intrinsic persistence, task-dependent astrocytic allocation and adaptive myelin/vascular changes expose real gaps. There is no established safe broadly beneficial manipulation direction for each.
7. **Capacity and reversal kinetics.** Task scores rarely identify global reserve. New-material performance after washout, prolonged sleep/physiological consequences and repeated-use compensation are sparsely measured. Eventual recovery of one recognition phenotype is not whole-state reversibility.
8. **The unexplored remainder.** Uncharacterized genes, cell-state transitions, receptor complexes, glial interactions and peripheral signals are not exhausted by this familiar-pathway map. Literature familiarity should not be the only discovery prior. Conversely, poor annotation is not evidence of headroom, useful direction, acute onset or reversibility.

Rolf's suspicion—that an large-scale lever may sit outside established literature—is therefore compatible with these findings. The map gives failure modes and constraints for discovery, not a proof that only familiar small effects remain possible. **Unknown mechanism, unused capacity and accessible control are three different unknowns.**

## 12. The approach axis: a familiar target can still be a new experiment

A **target** is the receptor, channel, enzyme, complex or relay being changed. An **approach** specifies which function, state, interaction, location or time window changes. Plain agonism/blockade can be graded and subtype-selective too; “blunt” is shorthand for an intervention that does not distinguish the particular function we need. Allostery is not automatically selective, safer or cognition-enhancing.

**Genuine novelty** would be an untested target–approach–context combination with a reason to improve the joint cognitive frontier: for example, changing a local signaling pool while sparing the same protein's useful action elsewhere. A new target name, binding-site structure or cellular marker does not establish this. Neither does repackaging a known allosteric modulator as a newly discovered mechanism.

| Approach class | What it can offer over undifferentiated agonism/blockade | Best located healthy above-baseline cognition evidence, at any target | Remaining limits against the full cognitive enhancement profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Positive allosteric modulation (PAM)** | Changes response through another binding site; a pure PAM can preserve endogenous input timing rather than continuously activate the target. Some modulators also have intrinsic agonist activity, so activity dependence must be demonstrated. | AMPA PAM CX717: acute normal-alert-monkey matching accuracy **74.9% → 90.5%**, with larger raw change on difficult trials [12, F]. The selective human PDE4-family memory results [3,4] are **not** evidence that an allosteric approach caused those results. | Preserving timing does not distinguish useful inputs from distractors. No broad healthy-human gain or complete state reversal. A supposed modulation ceiling is ligand/system-dependent, not a universal safety guarantee. |
| **Negative allosteric modulation (NAM)** | Attenuates rather than necessarily abolishes endogenous responses; may distinguish receptor subunits and reduce unwanted inhibition without blocking all GABA signaling. | α5-selective negative GABA-A modulation improved intact-rodent water-maze performance in the inspected review [16, F; size/onset/recovery NR]. Its healthy-human example reversed alcohol impairment, **not** intact-baseline enhancement. | Reducing inhibition can disrupt timing, increase anxiety or promote excessive excitation. Healthy knockout gains do not validate an acute NAM. Strong or poorly selective modulation may still behave like broad blockade. |
| **Subtype / splice-isoform selectivity** | Spares related proteins with useful or adverse functions; splice variants may offer a further anatomical distinction. | α5-negative modulation above is a pharmacological example. GluN2B overexpression [11] and PDE8B inactivation [97] give intact-mouse target-specific gains, but are constitutive genetics, not demonstrations of a selective acute drug. Sizes NR for these genetic studies. | Subtype ≠ cell/compartment; peripheral expression and developmental compensation matter. PDE8B loss is associated with anxiety in mice and damaging human striatal degeneration [97,101]. Human PDE8B brain/thyroid splice differences are real [99], but selective control of those variants is not established. |
| **Conformational / activity-state selectivity** | Favors activated, phosphorylated, stressed or assembled protein states rather than all copies. Could restrict action to the state that is actually limiting processing. | ISRIB provides an assembly-state example: it promotes active eIF2B complex assembly [83, A], with intact-mouse weak-training consolidation gains [34, F] and null/harmful outcomes [35,36]. Molecular administration is acute; the demonstrated gain emerges later, not as a broad waking state. | There is no automatically “good” active state: stress responses and LTD have useful functions. The favorable task/stress window, reversibility and transfer must be established independently. |
| **Biased signaling / functional selectivity** | Preferentially routes a receptor's output toward one downstream pathway rather than proportionally increasing all outputs. Bias must be measured relative to a reference ligand while separating receptor reserve, efficacy, assay amplification and tissue context [84, A]. | **No clean healthy above-baseline cognitive gain causally attributed to quantified ligand bias was located in this scoped review.** Receptor-specific gains, psychedelic plasticity or behavioral dissociations alone do not demonstrate bias. | Bias in an engineered cell assay can disappear or reverse in brain tissue. The desired pathway balance for learning versus working memory is unknown; apparent safety advantages may reflect lower efficacy instead of true bias. |
| **Activity / use dependence** | Changes signaling preferentially when a synapse or channel is being used; open-channel blockade can spare inactive channels. This is related to, but not identical with, conformational state selectivity. | AMPA facilitation is the strongest positive acute intact-baseline example here [12]. Use-dependent NMDA blockade is an established pharmacological approach [86, A], but a healthy-person systematic review found small/inadequate memantine studies, not convincing broad enhancement [87, A]. | More use can mean distractors or already overactive circuits. Use dependence is not correctness dependence. Blockade can suppress useful plasticity; positive modulation can destabilize activity. No full-profile result. |
| **Protein–protein interaction (PPI), complex assembly or localization modulation** | Can change a scaffold, dimer or partner-specific signaling pool while retaining catalytic activity or other functions; stabilizing an interaction can be as relevant as disrupting it. | eIF2B stabilization by ISRIB [83] links this approach to the healthy-mouse evidence in §5.1. PP1 genetic inhibition is an intact-mouse brake result [76], but it is **not** direct validation of a selective small-molecule PPI approach. PDE11A4 homodimer disruption already improved aged-mouse social memory without altering catalytic activity [81]; that is restoration, not healthy-young enhancement. | Native complex abundance, causal partner identity, intracellular accessibility and recovery are unresolved. Viral expression in the PDE11 study is not a reversible drug state. “PPI modulation” is not an untried cognition class. |
| **Regulated protein degradation / stabilization** | Changes protein abundance, including noncatalytic scaffold functions; may distinguish complexes or cell types if recruitment machinery permits. It is event-driven rather than requiring continuous occupancy. | **No healthy above-baseline cognition gain from a selective induced degrader was located.** Neurological TPD literature inspected here concerns disease-oriented strategies [85, A]. Knockout enhancement is not evidence for acute controlled degradation. | Whole-protein removal may delete useful functions. BBB entry, cell-specific degradation machinery and resynthesis determine onset/recovery. Removing the initiating agent need not restore protein abundance; this is a particularly poor default fit to a fast reversible state. |
| **Compartment / cell / phase targeting and closed-loop control** | Restricts otherwise familiar modulation to the circuit, intracellular pool or encoding/retrieval phase that needs it; state sensing could avoid fixed global settings. These are enabling approaches, not a single pharmacological mechanism. | Mouse astrocyte activation during learning improved next-day recall [48, A]; in-phase spindle induction improved consolidation [67, A]. Human EEG-driven good-versus-poor presentation is proof of state-contingent memory control [72, A], **not** demonstrated gain over normal timing at equal elapsed time. | Brain entry does not confer circuit entry. Genetic preparation, delivery or waiting can be the hidden cost. No evidence here for an acute reversible broad healthy-human controller. |

These classes overlap: one intervention may be allosteric, subtype-selective, use-dependent and complex-stabilizing. Their evidence must not be counted as four independent successes. An allosteric enzyme inhibitor also has intact-mouse memory evidence [2, F; size NR, some behavioral analyses disagree], but the inspected human trial is fragile-X treatment [5, A], not healthy enhancement. Greater molecular precision could remove a tradeoff; it cannot supply missing information, correct credit assignment or prove unused cognitive capacity.

**What this map does and does not enumerate.** It covers requested signaling/druggable *classes* and explicit examples, including all PDE families. It is **not** a gene-complete kinase, phosphatase, GPCR/orphan-receptor, transporter or chromatin inventory. Unlisted targets are unchecked, not untested. A full inventory should start with HGNC/Pharos and curated pharmacology, retaining noncanonical complexes and peripheral relays separately; §14 explains the join. An assertion that named targets were “tested only through blunt modulation” is stronger than the evidence here. The narrower defensible statement is that the **identified** PPM1E/SLC25A51 work lacks a selective adult cognition perturbation, and the identified PDE8 cognitive experiments use knockout or inhibitors rather than demonstrated splice/pool-specific control. PDE11A4, AMPA and eIF2B are explicit counterexamples to the “only blunt” premise.

## 14. Ranking untested mechanisms with public data, without an obscurity bonus

**Rank the pair, not the unfamiliar name.** Keep five separate evidence dimensions: adult relevance, useful perturbation direction, target/brain access, expected state kinetics, and cognitive breadth/tradeoffs. Keep search completeness and annotation missingness as separate fields. An understudied gene receives **no positive rank contribution** merely for few papers, low Unknome knownness [91], Pharos Tdark status or a missing annotation. Missingness widens uncertainty; it does not establish importance.

A laptop-scale workflow can build on the existing data layer rather than rerun Route 2:

**Eligibility before ordering.** A strict brain-druggable shortlist needs positive evidence for adult relevant expression/function **and** credible selective target engagement/access. Then prioritize combinations supported by more than one independent evidence type, an adult rather than solely developmental rationale, and a plausible way to avoid known counter-direction effects. Use a qualitative/Pareto comparison rather than inventing a weighted cognitive enhancement score: if one candidate has stronger relevance but weaker access, preserve that tradeoff instead of hiding it in a scalar. Check sensitivity to expression dropout, ambiguous joins, ancestry and task choices. None of these eligibility checks defines a numerical threshold for “large.”

**What the existing layer actually supports:** strict `derived/cognition-hypotheses.tsv` has **zero direct common/rare convergence rows**. KDM5B and NACC1 converge only with the separate educational-attainment proxy; neither is a dark-gene discovery. PPM1E has common direct-cognition support (including Savage MAGMA **p = 4.3748 × 10⁻¹³**) but no qualifying rare hit, and is **Tbio**, not a verified chemical target. Its positional/MAGMA evidence is not target causality or useful inhibition direction. PDE8A/B, PDE11A, GPR158 and SLC25A51 are absent from this ledger—not absent from biology or cognition literature. See [README](../README.md) for mapping/QC and [synthesis Route 2](synthesis.md) for discovery limits.

**Result, not a concealed promise:** this atlas does **not** establish a nonempty list of never-studied, brain-expressed, selectively brain-druggable targets. The data joins needed to certify that list remain partial. The provisional ranking below is an order for **testing research hypotheses**, based on positive mechanistic evidence; it is not an executed public-data target ranking, a treatment shortlist or a probability of cognitive enhancement. If a strict chemical/brain-access gate is enforced now, the novel pairs below are not yet validated as eligible.
