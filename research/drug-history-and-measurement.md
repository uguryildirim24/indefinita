# Cognitive-drug history, failed translation and measurement

## Verdict

No drug in the evidence reviewed establishes the requested combination: onset within minutes to hours, full reversibility, a large improvement across learning, memory, reasoning and attention, in healthy people. Acute enhancement is real in some domains, but the strongest healthy-adult meta-analytic evidence is for small, selective effects. Feeling more capable, staying awake, spending longer on a problem, reversing a deficit and slowing dementia are different outcomes. None can substitute for the whole broad-improvement profile.

The failure record is more informative than a catalogue of promising receptors. Repeated phase 2-to-phase 3 reversals, failures despite biochemical target engagement, and occasional cognitive worsening show that a plausible mechanism or an improved animal memory task is not enough. Conversely, newer Alzheimer antibodies and a muscarinic-treatment signal in cognitively impaired schizophrenia mean that “all cognitive pharmacology has failed” is false. Their benefits do not establish healthy enhancement.

A genuinely large, broad, acute effect should be detectable with existing cognitive science. The problem is not a missing intelligence scanner. It is designing blinded comparisons with sufficient task difficulty, reliable measures, unfamiliar material, realistic transfer tasks, and follow-up after the acute state ends. There is no scientifically validated numerical definition of “broad improvement”; this report does not invent one.

## Scope and verification

This is a targeted narrative evidence review, not an exhaustive systematic review or a registry census. Sources were opened on 4 October 2026 using curl against Europe PMC or NCBI PMC. Every numbered source below is marked verified, with the access depth stated. Most primary trial results were checked in indexed abstracts, not paywalled full texts; full-text passages were opened for the principal historical, translational and effect-size discussions. A verified abstract verifies its reported results, not every detail of its methods or risk of bias.

Coverage is complete for the requested analytical questions: discovery routes, representative major failures in CIAS and Alzheimer disease, controlled healthy enhancement evidence, animal-to-human translation, measurement, target-selection lessons, and a ranked broad-improvement comparison. Corpus coverage is partial: this is not every historical compound, every unpublished trial, or every current development program. Participant-level data, inaccessible supplements and a complete accounting of unpublished failures were unavailable to this review. No numerical overall probability of discovering broad improvement is inferred from the material.

The focus is drug-development history and measurement. Mechanistic headroom, acute plasticity, devices and public-data discovery routes belong to the companion reports. The measurement principles apply equally to a drug, device or other intervention; the drug ranking below is not a ranking of all possible non-pill approaches. Rolf's dry-lab constraint matters: he can critically analyse the evidence and measurement methods, but an intervention's human efficacy and reversibility cannot be established by computation alone. No molecule design, preparation, dosing, sourcing or self-experimentation is proposed.

## 1. How cognitive-active drugs were actually discovered

### Clinical observation often came before a cognitive theory

Stimulants did not emerge from a successful rational search for general intelligence. Charles Bradley's 1937 observation was a clinical accident: benzedrine was being tried for headaches following neurological investigations. It did little for the headaches, but some children became better able to do schoolwork and regulate behaviour. Subsequent systematic observation pursued that signal. These were children with behavioural and neurological problems, not evidence of a universal enhancement in healthy adults. Methylphenidate was first synthesized in 1944 and marketed in 1954, initially across indications including fatigue, depressive states and narcolepsy, before its role in ADHD became central. These historical facts are described in Lange and colleagues' review, rather than verified here against Bradley's original records. [1]

Modafinil likewise developed from a wakefulness phenotype rather than an intelligence target. The historical account describes adrafinil's identification in 1974, inconsistent early clinical experience with it, identification of modafinil as an active metabolite, and observations in narcolepsy and idiopathic hypersomnia that led to controlled development. [2] The useful phenotype was reduced pathological sleepiness. An effective wake-promoting treatment subsequently became an enhancement candidate, but its original success does not show a rise in general cognitive capacity.

These histories favour observing consequential behaviour rather than trusting a receptor story. They do not endorse uncontrolled anecdotes as evidence: the observation generates the hypothesis; blinded comparisons determine whether it survives.

### Deficit biology plus screening produced symptomatic dementia treatments

Donepezil is a particularly clear documented discovery chain. Cholinergic neuronal loss in Alzheimer disease supplied the rationale. Existing acetylcholinesterase inhibitors had practical drawbacks: physostigmine's short duration and tacrine's liver toxicity. Sugimoto and colleagues describe a random-screening hit with cholinergic behaviour in rats, subsequent optimization of inhibitory activity and overall pharmacological balance, and eventual clinical evaluation on cognitive and clinician-rated outcomes. [3] This was neither pure serendipity nor pure target-first design: biological rationale, empirical screening and iterative development interacted.

The eventual benefit was real but limited. The 2018 Cochrane review included 30 studies with 8,257 participants. In its main comparison, the reported ADAS-Cog difference after 26 weeks was −2.67 points, with a 95% confidence interval of −3.31 to −2.02; the MMSE difference was 1.05 points. The authors describe small cognitive, daily-function and global-clinical benefits. More exposure did not simply yield more efficacy: the higher-strength comparison did not show additional efficacy and had more adverse events and withdrawals. [9] These are disease-treatment effects over weeks, not acute healthy-person intelligence effects, and those scale points are not IQ points.

Memantine illustrates repurposing. Van Marum's historical review describes synthesis in the early 1960s for a different intended indication, subsequent neurological investigation, an initially negative small Alzheimer study, and later controlled dementia trials supporting approvals in Europe in 2002 and the United States in 2003. [4] Its pharmacology is moderate-affinity uncompetitive NMDA-receptor antagonism—not indiscriminate amplification of glutamate signalling. The 2019 Cochrane review finds a small clinical benefit in moderate-to-severe Alzheimer disease but no benefit in mild disease. [10] That stage dependence directly contradicts the inference that a drug helping more severely impaired brains should necessarily enhance healthy ones.

### The nootropic label preceded strong demonstration of the ambitious claim

Giurgea introduced the term “nootropic” in the early 1970s. Piracetam became its defining example; it is chemically related to GABA, but that does not establish a simple GABA-receptor mechanism for cognitive enhancement. Reviews discuss membrane and multiple neurotransmitter effects rather than one securely validated healthy-enhancement target. [5,6]

The evidential history is contested rather than uniformly positive. A favourable pharmacological review [6] sits alongside a Cochrane review finding that global impressions did not translate into convincing benefits on specific cognitive measures and documenting inaccessible or unusable crossover data. [7] A 2024 meta-analysis of memory-impaired adults included 18 studies and 886 patients: its memory estimate was SMD 0.75, but the confidence interval was −0.19 to 1.69 and heterogeneity was I² = 96%. [8] The large-looking point estimate is not established efficacy, still less an acute healthy-person effect. It demonstrates why a marketed category, a plausible mechanism and a pooled headline number cannot carry the broad-improvement claim.

### Modern target-led development improved mechanistic precision, not certainty of efficacy

Later programs often started with a causal hypothesis: cholinergic receptor modulation for attention and memory, NMDA-related signalling for schizophrenia cognition, AMPA facilitation for learning, cyclic-nucleotide signalling for plasticity, or amyloid biology for Alzheimer disease. They then moved through biochemical assays, animal behaviour, exposure and safety studies, small human trials and confirmatory trials. The failure cases below show that each transition asks a different question.

For example, a phase 1 study of the PDE9A inhibitor BI 409306 demonstrated central exposure and increased cerebrospinal-fluid cGMP in healthy volunteers. That was credible evidence of pharmacological action, not evidence of better thinking. Subsequent phase 2 Alzheimer studies did not improve cognition. [23,24] The development lesson is to keep separate: reaching the brain, engaging a target, changing a physiological marker, improving a task, generalizing across tasks, and delivering useful real-world benefit.

## 2. CIAS: a long history of promising starts and failed confirmation

Cognitive impairment associated with schizophrenia is not synonymous with positive psychotic symptoms, negative symptoms, sedation or low motivation. A treatment can improve one without improving the others. The MATRICS initiative created a standardized way to measure cognitive change, making trials more comparable; it did not make cognition easy to treat. [39,42,43]

### Glycine transport: a particularly strong recent negative result

The GlyT1-inhibitor strategy aimed to enhance NMDA-related signalling through glycine availability. In the 2021 phase 2 BI 425809 study, 509 patients were randomized. Reported standardized effects on the MCCB composite were 0.34 and 0.30 for the two most promising groups after 12 weeks. [12]

BI 425809 became iclepertin. The published 2025 CONNEX report describes three phase 3 trials across 338 centres in 41 countries, with 1,835 treated participants. At 26 weeks the adjusted drug-placebo difference in MCCB composite T-score was 0.127, 95% CI −0.396 to 0.650; p = 0.63. It was not associated with significant cognitive improvement. [13]

This is stronger evidence against the tested treatment than an underpowered single-site null. The earlier result was small-to-moderate even before it failed confirmation. Differences in population, adherence, sites, measurement or treatment context can be investigated, but cannot retroactively convert this program into demonstrated efficacy. It is not proof that every form of NMDA modulation is futile; it is a strong reason not to treat GlyT1 inhibition itself as an established cognitive-enhancement route.

### Alpha-7 nicotinic agonists: subgroup rescue and early biomarkers were not enough

Encenicline's phase 2 schizophrenia trial randomized 319 patients and reported a small positive primary cognitive effect in one treatment group, Cohen's d = 0.257, alongside signals on other outcomes. [14] The later report of two phase 3 trials, involving 1,520 patients, found no significant advantage on the cognitive or functional co-primary measures. Importantly, all groups improved, including from screening to baseline, and repeated MCCB administrations did not eliminate continued learning effects. [15] This source is a conference abstract, not a full phase 3 paper; its evidential detail is correspondingly limited.

ABT-126 offers a clean warning about promising subgroups. Its initial study did not show a significant overall composite benefit, but nonsmokers appeared to benefit, prompting a biologically plausible smoking-status interpretation. [16] The dedicated nonsmoker phase 2b trial randomized 432 participants and failed: composite improvements were +2.66 on active treatment versus +2.46 on placebo in the principal comparison, with no significant functional benefit. [17] A separate smoker trial also failed to demonstrate a procognitive effect. [18] A subgroup is a hypothesis to prospectively retest, not a loophole around the whole-sample result.

Target engagement also remains a genuine concern. A 2024 pig PET study found substantial alpha-7 occupancy with TC-5619 but more limited occupancy with encenicline. [41] This does not establish what happened in human trial participants; it illustrates why affinity in a dish cannot substitute for exposure and occupancy at the clinical target. Neither the failure of one agonist nor a pig occupancy result justifies declaring all nicotinic mechanisms dead. Repeated alpha-7 failures do justify substantially downgrading unqualified claims for that approach.

### AMPA modulation: the pilot effect did not survive a larger test

CX516, an ampakine, entered clinical work after animal learning and memory effects. A small clozapine add-on pilot reported moderate-to-large between-group effects on attention and memory. [19] In the subsequent 105-patient randomized study, there was no advantage on the cognitive composite or any cognitive test at the principal or follow-up assessment. [20]

This does not adjudicate every later AMPA modulator, exposure profile or population. It does refute the shortcut from “facilitates synaptic learning” or “large pilot effect” to broad clinical cognitive enhancement. It also exemplifies the need for an independently replicated effect before a small trial's magnitude drives expectations.

### A meaningful counterexample: muscarinic treatment signals, with tight boundaries

Xanomeline/trospium deserves attention rather than omission. In pooled data from two phase 3 trials in acute schizophrenia, the full cognitive sample, N = 357, showed no significant treatment effect. A prespecified baseline-impaired subgroup did show a benefit: d = 0.54, rising to d = 0.80 under a more stringent baseline-impairment definition. These were five-week inpatient trials. [21]

That is more substantial than another unreplicated post hoc signal, but it still does not establish acute enhancement in healthy people, broad reasoning gains, or full reversibility. The impaired-subgroup result is not the full-sample effect. Minimal correlation with concurrent symptom improvement supports investigating a separable cognitive action, but lack of correlation is not definitive causal proof of independence from all clinical recovery effects. The paper itself calls for controlled work in stable cognitively impaired patients.

Nor does this rescue every muscarinic strategy: the selective M1 positive allosteric modulator MK-7622 failed its 240-participant Alzheimer proof-of-concept trial, which stopped for futility and had more cholinergic adverse events and treatment discontinuations on active treatment. [22] “Selective” and “allosteric” are pharmacological properties, not guarantees of useful cognition.

### What CIAS failures do and do not establish

A syndrome with marked impairment supplies headroom, but also heterogeneous pathology, antipsychotic co-treatment, smoking, illness instability and functional limitations. These can complicate detection. They are not evidence that a treatment secretly has a large effect in healthy people. A healthy-enhancement claim needs healthy-person data.

Likewise, a negative-symptom or psychosis trial must not be relabelled a negative cognition trial. Here, the principal examples use cognitive endpoints. Patient-reported concentration, a symptom-scale cognition factor, a performance battery and everyday functioning should be reported separately. A reproducible benefit on one does not authorize claims about all four.

## 3. Alzheimer trials: failed symptomatic targets, failed disease modification, and genuine but limited success

The often-quoted 99.6% failure figure comes from Cummings and colleagues' analysis of the 2002–2012 Alzheimer development period, which covered 413 trials. [11] It is a historical estimate from a particular pipeline analysis, not a current universal failure rate, not the probability that any one target is wrong, and not a calculation of the probability of broad improvement. Trials, compounds, approvals and unique mechanisms are different denominators.

### Symptomatic cognitive targets can fail despite strong rationale

BI 409306 changed CSF cGMP in phase 1. [23] Two phase 2 studies in prodromal and mild Alzheimer disease randomized 457 patients and found no benefit on the prespecified neuropsychological composite or secondary outcomes. [24] Thus, increasing a signalling molecule involved in plasticity is not itself evidence of beneficial network computation.

Idalopirdine, a 5-HT6 antagonist, failed three randomized trials comprising 2,525 patients when added to cholinesterase-inhibitor treatment. Cognition did not improve relative to placebo over 24 weeks. [25] Along with MK-7622, this is a counterweight to interpreting animal transmitter release, receptor selectivity or small earlier signals as a reliable route to clinical cognition.

These trials tested disease populations, often on background treatment. They cannot establish impossibility in every other context. They do lower the priority of simply repeating the same target rationale without a specific, testable explanation for the prior failure.

### Lowering a pathology marker can be neutral or harmful

Semagacestat inhibited gamma-secretase to reduce amyloid production. Its 1,537-patient phase 3 trial was stopped early. Cognition worsened in all groups and did not improve relative to placebo; the higher-exposure group had significantly worse daily functioning. Active treatment also produced more skin cancers, infections, serious adverse events and discontinuations. [26] It would overstate the primary abstract to say every cognitive comparison was statistically significant: the reported ADAS-Cog comparisons were not. The functional and safety harm is nevertheless clear.

This was not simply “animals worked, humans did not.” The animal-model review describes earlier warning signs that presenilin/gamma-secretase has vital functions beyond amyloid processing. [40] Some failures reflect ignoring relevant animal biology while celebrating a favourable efficacy assay. A target's non-cognitive physiological roles can make it unsuitable even when it moves the desired biomarker.

Verubecestat, a BACE1 inhibitor, did not improve prodromal Alzheimer disease. The 1,454-participant trial was stopped for futility; some clinical outcomes were worse on treatment. At 104 weeks, estimated CDR-SB worsening was 2.02 in the higher-exposure group versus 1.58 on placebo. [27] Moving earlier in the disease did not automatically solve the problem.

Solanezumab's A4 trial went earlier still: participants were cognitively unimpaired older adults with elevated amyloid. Among 1,169 randomized participants, treatment did not slow cognitive decline over 240 weeks. The PACC difference was −0.30, 95% CI −0.82 to 0.22. Amyloid accumulation was smaller on treatment, but cognition was not better. [28] This was prevention in a biomarker-selected population, not acute enhancement in healthy young adults. It also prevents “all earlier failures were just too late” from becoming an unfalsifiable explanation. Solanezumab targets monomeric amyloid; its failure does not settle the effects of every amyloid species or treatment.

### Do not erase the antibody successes—or confuse them with enhancement

Lecanemab's Clarity AD trial randomized 1,795 participants with early symptomatic Alzheimer disease. CDR-SB worsened by 1.21 on treatment and 1.66 on placebo over 18 months, a difference of −0.45, 95% CI −0.67 to −0.23. Amyloid burden declined, and the clinical result was statistically positive. Amyloid-related imaging abnormalities with edema or effusions occurred in 12.6% of treated participants. [29]

Donanemab's TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 randomized 1,736 participants. At 76 weeks, the low/medium-tau population had iADRS decline of −6.02 on treatment versus −9.27 on placebo, a difference of 3.25. The combined population also declined in both groups. Edema/effusion imaging abnormalities occurred in 24.0% on treatment versus 2.1% on placebo, and three deaths in the treatment group versus one on placebo were considered treatment-related. [30]

These are genuine examples of less decline, not restoration above baseline. They contradict a blanket claim that amyloid-directed clinical research achieved nothing. They fail the broad-improvement profile on population, time course, enhancement rather than preservation, and rapid reversibility. A relative slowing percentage must never be presented as the same percentage increase in intelligence or memory.

## 4. What enhancement trials in healthy people actually show

### Meta-analysis supports small selective effects, not zero effects and not broad improvement

Roberts and colleagues' 2020 meta-analyses concerned acute administration in healthy, non-sleep-deprived adults. Across 47 studies, the reported overall SMD was 0.12 for modafinil and 0.21 for methylphenidate. Modafinil improved memory updating, SMD 0.28. Methylphenidate effects were concentrated in recall, SMD 0.43; sustained attention, SMD 0.42; and inhibitory control, SMD 0.27. No overall effect was found for d-amphetamine in that analysis. [31]

Those numbers are reported estimates, not a universal ranking of medicines or people. Tests, exposures, samples and outcome definitions differ. An overall standardized average across heterogeneous measures is not a direct measurement of a change in general intelligence. Small studies and uncertain ecological transfer also constrain interpretation. Nevertheless, the scale is plainly unlike large gains across all requested domains.

Nicotine supplies evidence that not every apparent stimulant benefit is withdrawal reversal. A meta-analysis restricted to healthy nonsmokers or smokers with no or minimal deprivation reported positive effects in selected motor, attention and memory outcomes, with effect sizes from 0.16 to 0.44. [35] It did not demonstrate broad reasoning enhancement or establish an acceptable long-term healthy-enhancement risk-benefit balance.

Caffeine is an important benchmark, not a broad-improvement substitute. A 2025 meta-analysis of rested healthy adults included 31 trials and 1,455 participants, reporting g = 0.27 for attention accuracy and g = 0.28 for reaction time. [36] Better vigilance is real and useful; it is not evidence of general intellectual transformation. Habitual consumption and withdrawal remain variables to measure rather than assume away.

### Subjective enhancement can detach from objective performance

Ilieva and colleagues studied mixed amphetamine salts using a blinded placebo-controlled crossover design and 13 cognitive measures, including memory, inhibitory control, creativity, intelligence and scholastic achievement. They found no overall enhancement of the tested cognitive abilities, but participants believed the active capsule had enhanced them more than placebo. [32]

This is not evidence that every report of benefit is imaginary. It is direct evidence that subjective confidence is an unreliable proxy for objective enhancement. A study should record perceived benefit, confidence, task enjoyment and willingness to persist precisely because they can move without the cognitive endpoint.

### More work is not necessarily better work

Bowman and colleagues' randomized crossover experiment with 40 healthy adults used a knapsack optimization task. Relative to placebo, the drugs studied reduced attained solution value and reduced productivity, despite increased effort on relevant measures. The probability of reaching the optimal solution was not significantly reduced. [37] The full text is more nuanced than “every drug increased time”: the modafinil time-spent comparison was not significant, although productivity reductions were reported across the drugs. This is one stylized problem, not proof of impaired performance in every real-world task.

The chess trial is an equally instructive boundary case. In 39 male players, treatment increased reflection time and games lost on time. When all games were analysed, there was no performance benefit. Modafinil and methylphenidate looked beneficial only after accounting for game duration or excluding games lost on time. [38] Excluding time-outs changes the practical question. If a person cannot finish the exam, decision or work task by its deadline, a better partial solution is not automatically successful enhancement.

The measurement lesson is to retain accuracy, completion, time, errors and output quality. Neither reaction speed alone nor hours worked alone is sufficient.

### Dementia drugs have mixed healthy-person evidence, not a hidden established breakthrough

A systematic review of acetylcholinesterase inhibitors and memantine found small, heterogeneous healthy-person studies: selected benefits, null results, sleep-deprivation rescue and some transient negative effects. It concluded the evidence was inadequate for firm claims about neuroenhancement. [33] A separate acute donepezil study reported improvements in several recall measures and a backward-digit-span effect near the expected plasma peak. [34]

The positive study should not be suppressed. Nor should it be generalized into replicated, large, broad enhancement. Testing at the wrong time can miss a genuine effect; testing many outcomes and highlighting selected successes can exaggerate one. The correct response is prespecified timing and independent confirmation, not choosing whichever study matches a preferred story.

## 5. Why animal cognitive enhancement fails to translate

There is no single explanation, and the reason for a particular failed trial is often not established. The distinctions below are supported by the translational reviews and the clinical examples, with methodological implications stated as synthesis rather than as experimentally proven explanations for every compound. [39,40]

First, reversal of an induced deficit is not enhancement above an intact baseline. A drug that reverses scopolamine- or NMDA-antagonist-induced impairment may be opposing the artificial insult. That can establish useful pharmacology without predicting broad benefit in an uninjured brain. A disease-model rescue and a healthy-enhancement claim need separate evidence.

Second, the behavioural label may conceal a different construct. The CIAS translation review explains that spontaneous T- or Y-maze alternation can depend on habituation; radial-arm-maze performance can improve through a turning strategy; and two-position delayed tasks can be solved by positioning the body toward the later answer. Novel-object recognition often relies on familiarity rather than the recollection processes most relevant to the clinical deficit. [39] Calling all these results “working memory” or “episodic memory” does not make them equivalent to human measurements.

Third, behaviour is affected by perception, movement, novelty preference, anxiety and reward engagement. A faster or more exploratory animal need not have stored more information. Consequently, an apparent cognitive gain needs evidence separating memory or decision quality from those task demands. The modafinil discovery history and the human effort studies underscore the same broader warning: gross behavioural activity is an ambiguous readout. [2,37,39]

Fourth, pathology models capture only pieces of a human disease. Many Alzheimer transgenic models emphasize familial mutations, while most clinical disease is sporadic. Some do not reproduce the extent of human neuronal loss. A treatment that clears a modeled pathology before extensive injury may not restore function after human circuits have been lost. [40] This mismatch also cuts the other way: failure in a diseased older population is not a direct test of healthy acute enhancement.

Fifth, equivalent nominal exposure is not equivalent target engagement. Brain penetration, metabolism, receptor pharmacology and tissue context matter. The pig alpha-7 study is a concrete reminder of uncertain occupancy. BI 409306 demonstrates the complementary point: even central pharmacodynamic action does not guarantee clinical benefit. [23,24,41]

Sixth, chronic clinical conditions differ from simplified experiments. Background medicines, long-standing illness, age, smoking and the opportunity to use improved cognition affect outcomes. A treatment that enables learning may require learning opportunities; an immediate test and a repeated training regimen answer different questions. That possibility must be tested, not used to indefinitely excuse a failed endpoint.

Finally, sampling and analysis can inflate early promise. Small pilots, multiple tasks, selective reporting and incomplete blinding create opportunities for exaggerated effects. This review did not quantify their contribution to a specific animal literature and does not assign a made-up percentage of failure to “publication bias.” The CX516 and ABT-126 sequences show why replication and prospective subgroup confirmation matter even when the mechanism is plausible. [16–20]

Better translational tools exist. Touchscreen tasks with related demands across rodents, nonhuman primates and humans, including paired-associate learning and trial-unique delayed nonmatching, can reduce some construct mismatches. [39] They are improvements in inference, not validated broad-improvement detectors or a promise that future translation will succeed. Human evidence must still establish breadth, magnitude and usefulness.

## 6. Measuring the actual broad-improvement claim

### Define the outcome before selecting a flattering test

The claim is an acute increase in useful cognitive performance in rested healthy people, above their placebo/practice trajectory, across the specified domains—not merely treatment of disease or recovery from deprivation. A trial should estimate the effect during the proposed acute window, its domain profile, transfer to unfamiliar problems, the distribution of benefits and harms, and the trajectory after the intervention ends.

Broad improvement does not require every test to move identically, but it does require more than a composite driven entirely by processing speed or one memory test. Learning, delayed memory, reasoning and attention each need informative measurement. Working memory and executive control help interpret how those gains arise. Familiar knowledge and vocabulary are useful baseline descriptors, but are weak primary acute endpoints because a temporary state change does not automatically supply previously unlearned facts.

A task battery can show broad performance gains without proving a permanent change in latent general intelligence. A factor model requires sufficient data, stable measurement across conditions and no convenient post hoc redefinition of the factor. A single IQ score, online game or n-back result cannot settle the broader claim.

### Suitable test families, and their limits

For learning, use unfamiliar material with repeated acquisition opportunities and measure the learning curve, not only the final score. Parallel verbal lists, object-location associations or other well-characterized paired-associate tasks can separate how quickly material is acquired from its eventual retention. Record exposure time and the amount presented, so that additional effort is not mistaken for faster learning.

For memory, include delayed free recall and recognition with false-alarm information, alongside immediate performance. Free recall and recognition are not interchangeable. Distinguish encoding during the intervention from retrieval during it; superior retrieval while stimulated does not establish better stored memories. Test retention after the acute state has ended and consider whether sleep differences contributed.

For reasoning, use unfamiliar matrix, relational or multistep problems with enough difficult items for the enrolled population. Alternate forms need demonstrated comparability. Score correct solutions, partial progress, time and time-outs separately. A high score on an already memorized item bank is test familiarity, not evidence of general reasoning enhancement.

For attention, sustained-performance or vigilance tasks can measure lapses, accuracy, false alarms and reaction-time distributions. Include both sustained and selective demands rather than equating simple motor reaction time with all attention. A faster response that produces more mistakes is a trade-off, not an unqualified gain.

For working memory and executive control, combine tasks with distinct demands: complex span, updating, inhibition and switching where relevant. The n-back task is useful experimentally, but its reliability and its relationship to other working-memory measures limit its use as a solitary individual-difference or general-enhancement measure. [47]

Established batteries provide useful foundations. The MCCB was selected through explicit assessment of reliability, practice effects, relationships to functioning and practicality; it contains ten tests across seven domains, with co-norming described separately. [42,43] It is a strong CIAS benchmark, not automatically an optimized ceiling-free battery for high-performing healthy undergraduates or a complete assessment of the requested reasoning construct.

The NIH Toolbox adult validation provides another psychometric foundation. Reported composite test-retest correlations were 0.86–0.92, with evidence for convergent validity. [44] That supports reliability in its validation population; it does not prove sensitivity to an acute enhancement or freedom from ceiling effects in a selected high-ability group. CANTAB-style tasks can also supply standardized domain measures and cross-species links. [39] A brand name is not enough: reliability, score range, alternate forms and responsiveness must be checked for the exact task version and population.

Add transfer outcomes: learning unfamiliar course-like material, solving new problems, or completing realistic work under independently scored conditions. These should use blind scoring, explicit quality criteria and deadlines. They complement rather than replace psychometrically understood tasks. The knapsack and chess results show why ecological transfer cannot simply be assumed from a neuropsychological subtest. [37,38]

### Placebo, expectancy and unblinding

Randomized, blinded placebo comparison is the core causal contrast for a drug; an appropriately credible sham serves the same purpose for a device. Standardize instructions and recruitment language rather than advertising likely dramatic enhancement. Record baseline expectations and treatment guesses, preferably before outcome feedback, together with adverse effects that might reveal assignment.

An active placebo can sometimes improve blinding, but can itself change cognition or motivation. Its suitability needs evidence; it is not automatically superior to an inert comparator. Similarly, failure to guess assignment above chance does not by itself prove perfect blinding. Guessing correctly can follow a real effect, so deleting people who guessed correctly can introduce bias. Blinding assessment is diagnostic information, not an automatic statistical correction.

The drug-placebo contrast estimates the effect of assignment under that design. If unblinding changes expectations, distinguishing the molecule's direct action from those expectations may require an additional experimental manipulation and stronger assumptions. One cannot read that distinction directly from a confidence rating. Ilieva's result is the concrete warning: subjective benefit can appear without corresponding objective gains. [32]

### Practice and carryover

A before-and-after score increase is inadequate. Calamia and colleagues' meta-analysis assembled nearly 1,600 practice-effect estimates and identified influences including alternate forms, retest interval, age and clinical status. [45] Practice includes learned strategies and test sophistication, not just remembering answers. Switching the word list does not remove all of it.

Use familiarization, repeated baseline information where justified, equivalent novel forms, randomized/counterbalanced order, and a concurrently tested comparison condition. Estimate residual session effects rather than declaring that practice has stopped. Encenicline's phase 3 experience shows why repeated testing alone does not guarantee a plateau. [15]

A crossover can efficiently compare acute states within a person, but only when pharmacological and behavioural carryover are manageable. Washout of the intervention does not wash out learned strategies or retained material. If the treatment permanently improves learning about the task, later placebo performance is contaminated. Novel material and counterbalancing help, but a parallel-group confirmation may be necessary. Predefine period, sequence and missing-data analyses rather than patching the design after seeing a result.

### Ceiling effects, floor effects and unreliable difference scores

Screening measures such as the MMSE are designed to detect impairment, not discriminate large improvements at the high end of healthy performance. Normal-range or near-perfect baseline scores leave little room for gain. Simply selecting an extremely difficult task can create the opposite problem: floor performance, disengagement or dependence on specialized knowledge.

Use a broad difficulty range or validated adaptive item bank, inspect item-level distributions, and preserve comparable scaling across conditions. Adaptive testing must not make the intervention look effective merely by changing what is being measured. Analyse accuracy jointly with response time and monitor whether an apparent gain is a shift in speed-accuracy policy.

Hedge and colleagues' “reliability paradox” is important here. A classic cognitive task can produce a robust average experimental effect while poorly ranking individual differences; interference/difference scores are particularly vulnerable. [46] That does not make Stroop or stop-signal tasks useless. It means reliability for person-level response classification cannot be assumed from a famous group effect. Declaring an individual a drug “responder” requires repeated evidence beyond a noisy difference between two sessions.

### Effort, motivation and confidence

Motivation can produce objective score gains, not just pleasant feelings. Duckworth and colleagues' meta-analysis of incentive experiments reported an average increase in intelligence-test performance of 0.64 SD in low-stakes settings, with larger effects at lower baseline performance. [48] That does not show an equivalent improvement in underlying intelligence or predict the same effect in an already highly motivated sample.

Use comparable incentives, instructions, task duration and testing conditions. Measure persistence, response strategy, subjective effort, confidence calibration, fatigue and output quality alongside the cognitive endpoints. Compare demanding accuracy-limited tasks with self-paced tasks where extra time can purchase better performance. Where the mechanism question matters, an orthogonal manipulation of incentives or time constraints can be informative.

Do not merely regress out post-treatment motivation and announce a “pure cognition” effect: motivation may be part of the causal pathway, and conditioning on it can bias estimates. Report total useful performance effects and the evidence about possible mechanisms separately. Improved motivation that genuinely increases high-quality work is valuable, but must not be relabelled a large broad increase in learning and reasoning capacity.

### Acute onset and full reversibility are separate empirical claims

Assessment must sample the proposed onset and duration rather than only a single favourable time point. Match testing to plausible pharmacodynamic timing; plasma peak is a clue, not proof that cognitive effects peak simultaneously. The acute donepezil study demonstrates why timing can affect the result. [34]

After the acute period, assess new-material learning capacity, reasoning, attention, sleep, mood and adverse effects relative to the comparison trajectory. A nonsignificant post-treatment difference does not prove equivalence or full recovery: confidence intervals and a justified equivalence margin matter. Small studies cannot establish absence of uncommon or delayed harms.

There is an important conceptual distinction: if someone learned more while enhanced, retaining that knowledge is desirable and need not disappear when the state ends. Reversibility means the altered capacity/state and unwanted effects resolve, not that the person forgets what was learned. Tests of newly presented material after washout are more informative about capacity returning to baseline than retesting the material learned during treatment.

Short-lived blood levels do not establish full reversibility. Nor do transient EEG, pupil, fMRI or receptor-occupancy changes demonstrate cognitive enhancement. Such measures can establish timing or target engagement; behavioural benefit and recovery still need direct measurement.

### What effect size would count?

Use raw meaningful outcomes alongside standardized effects and confidence intervals. A standardized mean difference describes a difference relative to a specified variability denominator; it does not mean that cognition improved by the same numerical percentage. Report which denominator was used. Within-person d-z, between-person d and other repeated-measures effect sizes are not interchangeable. Lakens explains why a crossover's reduced error variance can yield a large standardized statistic without implying the same population shift as a large between-group effect. [49]

For orientation only, Cohen's conventional d values of 0.2, 0.5 and 0.8 are labelled small, medium and large. Lakens explicitly cautions that these are arbitrary benchmarks and should not be used rigidly. [49] They are not broad-improvement acceptance thresholds. The healthy acute drug averages of 0.12 and 0.21 are small; selected-domain estimates around those reported for recall or attention are larger but still not broad transformation. [31] Conversely, the d = 0.80 muscarinic subgroup result is numerically “large” by convention but is still a disease-subgroup result after weeks, with no demonstration of the other requested properties. [21]

No single d establishes broad improvement. The relevant evidence would be a replicated, substantial gain across the required domains, expressed in useful unfamiliar-task performance, clearly beyond placebo/practice and without compensating impairments, emerging in the requested acute window and resolving as specified. Practical anchors—how much novel material is learned and retained, which harder problems become solvable, and whether accurate work is completed within the same time—should determine the minimum effect worth claiming before data collection. There is no validated conversion from those outcomes to hypothetical broad improvement or a universal IQ-point target.

Power the study for those prospectively justified effects and for precision across the domain claims, using realistic reliability and within-person correlation. Do not design a tiny trial around the largest published pilot estimate. Prespecify a primary composite and domain analyses, control multiplicity, publish null and negative outcomes, and independently replicate. A small significant composite effect is not enough; neither is “not significant” enough to conclude no meaningful effect when the interval remains wide.

For an unusually large broad effect, adequate high-ceiling tasks should show a coherent signal without hundreds of exploratory comparisons. Existing tools are capable of detecting that kind of behaviour. Establishing dependable generalization, uncommon harms and full reversibility remains more demanding than detecting an initial score difference.

## 7. What kinds of target or development claim to deprioritize

First, deprioritize another undifferentiated alpha-7 agonist, GlyT1 inhibitor, 5-HT6 antagonist or generic cyclic-nucleotide enhancer justified only by the old animal or phase 2 rationale. The specific failure records require a new discriminating explanation and human evidence, not a fresh slogan. [13,15,17,18,24,25] This is a prioritization judgment, not a declaration that every possible intervention at those targets is impossible.

Second, avoid treating broad receptor activation or inhibition as a one-direction intelligence control. The harmful secretase results and mixed muscarinic results demonstrate physiological functions and tolerability constraints outside the desired readout. [22,26,27] Greater target engagement can increase toxicity rather than cognition.

Third, deprioritize pathology-removal strategies for this particular acute reversible healthy-enhancement goal. Alzheimer antibodies can benefit selected patients but are aimed at a different timescale and outcome. Their success does not bring them close to broad improvement. [29,30]

Fourth, reject biomarkers as stand-alone efficacy claims. CSF cGMP, amyloid clearance, electrophysiological changes and occupancy can validate parts of a mechanism while behaviour remains unchanged or worsens. [23,24,27–30,41]

Fifth, distrust nonspecific “neuroprotective,” “plasticity-promoting” or “multi-target” labels without a bridge to the specific human outcome. Piracetam's contradictory literature and CX516's pilot-to-larger-trial reversal are examples of why broad mechanistic language is not broad efficacy. [6–8,19,20]

Finally, reject rescuing a null program by endlessly changing endpoints, subgroups or definitions of success. Prospectively confirmed heterogeneity is valuable, as the muscarinic subgroup work suggests. Unreplicated subgroup selection is not. None of these lessons licenses the inference that a failed disease treatment must work in healthy people because their brains are less damaged.

## 8. Ranked leads closest to the broad-improvement profile in this evidence base

This is a qualitative ranking of evidential proximity, not treatment advice, a safety ranking, or proof of one drug's superiority. None qualifies. Full reversibility of a large broad effect is unestablished for every entry because the large broad effect itself has not been demonstrated.

1. **Methylphenidate: best-established acute healthy-person signal across several selected domains in the main comparative meta-analysis.** Acute onset is supported by acute studies; recall, sustained attention and inhibition can improve. Magnitude is small overall, breadth is incomplete, reasoning transformation is unestablished, and complex-task trade-offs exist. Acute testing does not establish complete recovery without sleep or other downstream effects. It is a reference case for the size and limitations of current enhancement, not a broad-improvement success. [31,37,38]

2. **Modafinil: clear acute wakefulness pharmacology, with limited rested-healthy cognitive gains.** Its development history firmly supports wake promotion; the rested-healthy overall SMD of 0.12 and updating effect of 0.28 do not establish broad large enhancement. Complex-task results depend on task and time constraints. The requested rapid and fully reversible large cognitive state is not established. [2,31,37,38]

3. **Nicotine and caffeine: reproducible narrow acute benchmarks.** Healthy-person meta-analyses show selected attention and memory effects for nicotine and attention effects for caffeine. Neither demonstrates broad reasoning and learning gains of broad-improvement magnitude. Nicotine's dependence relevance and the distinction between acute effects and longer-term risk mean “temporarily beneficial” is not equivalent to fully reversible or suitable. They rank for replicated acute human effects, not because they are hidden intelligence multipliers. [35,36]

4. **Muscarinic/cholinergic enhancement: an interesting clinical signal, not established broad improvement in healthy people.** Xanomeline/trospium has a replicated cognitive signal in a prespecified impaired schizophrenia subgroup, including a conventionally large estimate under a stricter impairment definition; healthy status, minutes-to-hours onset, broad reasoning transfer and full reversibility are not demonstrated. Donepezil has some positive healthy studies but mixed systematic evidence. The negative MK-7622 result prevents treating M1 pharmacology as generically validated. This is the more interesting residual development lead in this historical review, but ranks below acute healthy evidence for the actual requested profile. [21,22,33,34]

5. **AMPA-facilitating approaches: closer in mechanistic aspiration than in demonstrated clinical performance.** Animal learning effects and an early human signal explain the interest; CX516's larger negative trial substantially weakens direct efficacy inference. Large broad acute benefit in healthy people and its reversibility remain unestablished. Other approaches require their own evidence, not inheritance of the word “ampakine.” [19,20]

Piracetam, the failed GlyT1/alpha-7/5-HT6/PDE9 programs, and Alzheimer pathology-directed drugs do not earn higher ranks by having numerous mechanistic papers. Their respective problems are uncertain efficacy, failed confirmation, or a fundamentally different outcome and timescale. The useful conclusion is not to lower Rolf's goal to staying awake: it is that the reviewed record has not delivered his goal, and that credible future evidence must satisfy the whole profile rather than its easiest component.
